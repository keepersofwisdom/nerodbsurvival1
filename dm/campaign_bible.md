## 1. Themes (what the whole campaign is *about*)
1. **Who gets in.** A stabilization unit takes anyone (the Ley de Urgencia says so). A bunker
   takes a list. The founder's two projects are opposites, and he is both.
2. **Inheritance.** Power, money, guilt and concrete pass from the dictatorship's men to the
   founder to the heir. What do you owe the people who built the thing that saves you?
3. **The city above and the city below.** Santiago's class geography (the barrio alto and the
   poblaciones) is mirrored underground. The rich always had somewhere to go.
4. **Preparation versus living.** Every peso in the bunker is a peso not spent on a child, a
   ventilator, a holiday. Every night in the bunker is a night not at home.
5. **Medicine as triage of the world.** The skills of the ED (decide fast, accept loss, keep
   going) are the skills of the end, and they cost the soul the same way.

## 3. Secret truths (draft)

### 3.1 The Old Men (*los Viejos*; among themselves, **"la Mesa"**)
- **Origin: December 1978.** During the **Beagle crisis**, when Chile and Argentina came within
  hours of war (Operación Soberanía, 22 Dec 1978), Santiago practised blackouts and feared
  Argentine bombers. A handful of very rich, very connected men (around twelve at the start)
  decided that if the state would not shelter them, they would shelter themselves.
- **The engineers.** They used officers and NCOs of the army's engineering arm (the **Cuerpo
  Militar del Trabajo** and the engineers working on La Moneda's reconstruction and the
  civic district's security works, 1974–1981) and the cover of the great excavations of the
  time: **Metro Line 1 and Line 2** construction, building basements in Providencia, the
  expansions of private clinics. Spoil left in Metro trucks. Permits were never asked for.
- **Second wave, 1983–1987.** The *protestas* and the FPMR's arms (Carrizal Bajo, Aug 1986;
  the attempt on Pinochet, Sep 1986) made the Old Men fear revolution more than Argentina. They
  deepened and armed the shelters. **Some of them lent their spaces to the CNI** (rooms used to
  hold people). This is the bunkers' worst secret. *(Handle with weight: it connects the game
  to the real *detenidos desaparecidos*.)*
- **Transition, 1990–2005.** With democracy, the bunkers lost their purpose and gained risk.
  La Mesa met less. Several men died; their heirs know nothing. Maintenance lapsed. By 2005,
  perhaps **four** of the original men are alive (aged 75–90), and they have a problem: they
  are dying, the bunkers are rotting, and their families can't be told.
- **What they want from the founder:** someone young, competent, discreet, with a medical
  business that justifies digging, power, generators, oxygen and drug stores, and who shares
  their fear. They want **an heir**. Some want absolution. One wants him dead.

### 3.2 The bunkers (anchored to `research/03_underground_santiago.md`)
**Rule:** the bunkers are invented **beside** real places, never attributed to them. Real memorial
sites (Clínica Santa Lucía, SICAR's "El Hoyo", Borgoño) stay what they are. Real hospitals
(Hospital del Trabajador, the Posta, the Salvador) are neighbours, not owners.

**Engineering truths for all of them:** Santiago's gravel is dry and stable down to the water
table (~40–50 m in the centre), so a bunker at 15–30 m stays dry. Bunkers hide best **inside a
legal deep excavation** (parking levels, a "seismic retrofit", a radiotherapy "búnker", a
generator room); a standalone tunnel means hundreds of spoil trucks that someone notices.

### 3.3 The founder's end (to be settled with decision E)
Options, to be decided or kept secret:
- **Dies** (natural, accident, or murder for the bunkers) in 2024–25, perhaps on the night of the
  **2025 blackout**, perhaps leaving letters for the heir.
- **Disappears** (into a bunker, abroad, killed and hidden).
- **Early dementia** from ~2022: he forgets where things are and who knows; the heir must
  reconstruct his mind from notebooks.
- **Imprisoned** (fraud, the CNI rooms exposed, a death in a unit).
- **Chooses the heir** and lives, aged and diminished, as a guide with his own agenda.

## 6. Start hook (Arc 1, 2005)
Combine **The Wall**, **The Investor** and **The Patient Who Knew** as the player's choices
allow: the founder's money and site decisions in character creation determine which comes
first. Let the first weeks be ordinary (permits, hires, patients) and let the strangeness enter
slowly.

## 7. Divergence guidance
- The Old Men can't change national history: they're old, frightened and private. They can
  change *careers*, *permits*, *lawsuits*, *who dies in a clinic*.
- If the founder exposes the CNI rooms, it becomes a real national case (judges, families, the
  press, the Army). Reason it forward honestly.
- If the founder saves or loses famous patients, the ripples are local.

## 8. Possible endings (for the founder's era)
- **Best:** the Network thrives, the bunkers are ready, the secret is kept with honour (or
  revealed with justice), the heir is chosen well.
- **Mixed:** the Network survives, the founder doesn't, the heir inherits a mess.
- **Worst:** the founder becomes one of the Old Men.
