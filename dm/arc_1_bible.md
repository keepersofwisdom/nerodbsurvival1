## 2. The setup
- **How it begins:** after his last 24-hour shift at the Posta (Wed 30 → Thu 31 March 2005), he
  walks to the civic district to view buildings with the broker Mauricio Fierro.
- **What he knows:** his idea, his numbers (≈ 14,500 UF from four partners; a flagship needs
  ≈ 120,000–170,000 UF), that banks lend ~50–60% against property, that he wants the civic
  district (roads converge there: Alameda, Norte–Sur, Costanera; government workers by day;
  the Posta two kilometres away is drowning; offices are fleeing east, so prices are low).
- **The question this arc asks:** *what will he accept to get it built?*

## 3. The truth (secret)
- **Fierro is paid** by Inversiones Huelén to steer Bastías to the **Edificio Caja Austral** and
  make it look like a bargain. The seller (a "family estate") is a Larrañaga cousin's company:
  the price (62,000 UF for ≈ 4,200 m² + 2 basements) is below market on purpose.
- **Beneath the Caja Austral's second basement lies B2 "El Archivo"** (1980–84, ~12–15 m): a
  shelter for 40, damp in its north-east corner, La Mesa's archive (accounts, member lists,
  a copy of Benavente's plans for B1–B4, a 1985–87 ledger of "guests" in B3 written in code).
  A brick arch in basement −2, painted over, hides its stair. A gallery runs east towards the
  Plaza de la Constitución vaults; **in 1990 it was walled up** on La Mesa's side, and there
  are **footprints in the dust on the far side** (someone from the state side still walks there).
- **Pincheira**, the night watchman since 1990, guards the arch for La Mesa. He was the CMT
  sergeant who supervised the digging. He drinks pisco from a thermos and listens to Radio
  Cooperativa.
- **The lunch:** Salinas and Larrañaga will invite Bastías to the Club de la Unión within
  ~10 days of the viewing, whether or not he buys. They'll talk about Aceh, about his father
  ("un hombre muy serio, su papá"), about the bomb. They will offer Huelén's money on the
  conditions in `campaign_bible.md §3.1`. They will ask him to buy the Caja Austral and let
  Benavente design the "seismic and parking" works. They won't mention B2.
- **Rehbein**, who opposed bringing an outsider into La Mesa, works against him through his
  real-estate company's influence: the bank's credit committee is "advised" (Verónica Saldías
  knows), and later a DOM official sits on the building permit. He doesn't want Bastías dead
  yet; he wants him to fail, so that La Mesa's bunkers lapse and he can buy them.
- **Benavente** will plan the deep works to break into B2 from above "by accident": the
  excavation of the new third basement exposes the arch (Act 2), so that the doctor discovers
  the shelter as a fait accompli, already partly his.
- **Hernán Bastías**: Benavente asked him in 1987 to consult on shielding; he refused. If
  Rodrigo mentions Benavente or shielding specs, his father goes very quiet.

## 6. The discovery tree
- **Path A — he buys the Caja Austral with Huelén's money:** the works start mid-2005;
  Benavente's design; the arch appears in Sep–Oct; Pincheira takes him down; B2 with the
  archive (he can read the accounts and the member list; the 1985–87 ledger is coded); the 1990
  wall and the footprints. La Mesa then "explains" a little (Salinas): the shelters, the bomb,
  the list. Not B3's rooms.
- **Path B — he buys the Caja Austral without Huelén:** bank-only financing is thin (Rehbein's
  pressure): he must cut scope (no ICU at first) or bring another investor. Pincheira still
  guards the arch; La Mesa still wants him and will try again (Salinas's fall). The arch is
  found when *he* digs, or Pincheira shows him if he earns it.
- **Path C — he picks another building (Teatinos tower or the Moneda bank):** Huelén's offer
  still comes, now to buy the flagship's basement rights under his chosen building ("we'll pay
  for the deep works"). B2 stays dormant; La Mesa's interest becomes pressure; Fierro loses his
  fee and grows bitter.
- **Path D — he investigates the old men first** (Investigation, History & Politics; his father;
  Javiera; the Club's members' list; property registry): Inversiones Huelén is Larrañaga's family
  company; Larrañaga's businesses thrived in 1975–1989; Benavente was CMT; the Caja Austral's
  "family estate" is a Larrañaga cousin. Too much digging alerts Rehbein.
- **Unexpected paths:** improvise consistently with §3 and write it here.

## 8. The Network and the bunkers in this arc
- **Business pressures:** money gap; the bank; permits; partners' equity; hiring; the GES
  scramble; competitors' attention (a big clinic group notices a newcomer in the centre).
- **Bunker progress:** if Huelén funds, Benavente's design (deep works clock 8); B2 found
  (Exposure starts). His own cellar and the Quilicura drugs (Exposure risk 1).
- **Exposure risks:** spoil trucks after December 2005; Carolina's eyes on the plans; Javiera's
  research; Gonzalo's due diligence; the DOM's archived plans.

## 9. Red herrings and traps
- **Looks significant, isn't:** the Moneda bank's great vault (just a vault); the asbestos in the
  Teatinos tower (expensive, not sinister).
- **Is significant, doesn't look it:** Pincheira's thermos and radio; the name "Huelén"; the
  seller's company; the arch's fresh paint; the bank's sudden coldness.
- **Mistakes with consequences:** signing Huelén's documents without a lawyer (the "condiciones
  especiales" annex gives La Mesa a perpetual right of access to "las instalaciones
  subterráneas"); guaranteeing the bank loan with the family house without telling Carolina;
  reporting the arch to the Consejo de Monumentos (works stop for months, the press arrives,
  La Mesa turns cold).

## 11. Possible endings of the arc
- **Best:** Clínica Toesca opens by March 2006 on sound finance; Huelén's money on terms he
  controls; partners united; B2 found and kept quiet; Carolina an ally.
- **Good:** opens late or over budget; the secret kept; a partner bruised.
- **Mixed:** opens, but with a partner lost, debts tight, the marriage strained, or Javiera
  sniffing.
- **Bad:** no Caja Austral; a smaller unit in a rented casona; La Mesa turns to other plans.
- **Worst:** bankruptcy before opening, the house at risk, and the coded ledger in the wrong hands.

## 13. DM notes
- **Tone:** quiet, adult, hopeful and uneasy. A man building something good with money from men
  he shouldn't trust.
- **Themes to press:** what money costs; the centre of Santiago as a place of power and memory;
  the marriage as a partnership.
- **Sensory palette:** autumn smog over the Alameda, the yellow micros' diesel roar, the
  Nextel chirp, Fotolog on teenagers' screens, the plaza works' jackhammers, the Club's wood and
  silence, the cold damp of basement −2, the smell of pisco and Radio Cooperativa in the
  watchman's booth, Bachelet posters.
- **What makes it hard:** every money option has strings; the best building is the trap.
- **How it should change him:** from doctor to owner; from preparing alone to preparing with
  others; from honest to careful.
- **Seeds for later eras:** the coded ledger (B3); the footprints beyond the wall (the state's
  side, Era II's restoration); Benavente's plans for B1–B4; Hernán's 1987 refusal; Javiera's
  story. **The heir:** none needed yet (Pietro is 8); possibly a crypto-curious Nerod father at
  a dinner party years from now.
