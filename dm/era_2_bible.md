## 1. The truth Rodrigo never learned

### B3 "La Bodega" — Barrio Yungay
- A walled warehouse on a short street in **Barrio Yungay**, owned since 1984 by a company that
  passed from La Mesa to Pincheira's name in 1991 (La Mesa's way of keeping it off their books).
  When Pincheira died in 2019, his daughter **Rosa Pincheira** (58) inherited a "bodega" she has
  never opened; she rents the street front to a mechanic. Her son **Bastián** (31, Toesca's
  maintenance technician) knows from his grandfather's drunk stories that *"el Tata cuidaba dos
  sótanos"*.
- A passage runs from B3's lower room to the **Libertad station of Line 5**, finished and never
  opened (research/03). Metro inspects the station twice a year; the passage's door looks like a
  service hatch.
- **The rooms:** in 1985–87 La Mesa lent B3 to a CNI unit. Eleven people were held there over
  those years. Four died there. **Salinas signed one of the death certificates** (a "cardiac
  arrest" for a 29-year-old electrician, December 1985). Names are scratched in the plaster of one
  room. The 1980s rations, FAMAE arms and a CNI radio are still there.
- **"Yungay"**, Salinas's last word, meant this.

### The ledger
- The coded ledger (1985–87) is the list of B3's "guests": dates in, dates out, a code for the
  outcome (*T* traslado, *L* liberado, *F*…). The code is a simple substitution keyed to La
  Mesa's 1979 member list, which is in **B2's filing cabinets**. Rodrigo's three photographed pages
  (on CD in the Ñuñoa cellar) cover **November–December 1985**, the months of the electrician.
- The **original ledger** went from Benavente's study (2005) into his grandson's custody after
  2014 (§3).

### B4 "La Quinta" — La Reina
- Rehbein's private ark, maintained continuously, stocked for twenty people for a year. It was
  upgraded in 1999 and 2017. The family sheltered there during the 2020 lockdowns.
- It is built across the **San Ramón fault** trace. Its access shaft would shear in a large
  crustal quake.

### The far side of the 1990 wall
- The gallery from B2 continues to a 1930s service vault adjoining the real underground under
  the Plaza de la Constitución (research/03: SICAR's 1935 vault, the Agustinas 1220 car park, the
  1982–83 "búnker" hall). The footprints of 2005 belonged to La Moneda's drivers and a Carabineros
  maintenance detail, who use the vault as a store and smoking room. They don't know where the
  gallery goes; they think it's a dead end.
- **The 2025–26 restoration** of La Moneda's bunker hall (real: MOP, the Pío V contract) puts
  contractors in that vault. One of them finds the 2006 concrete plug and a 1980s vent that
  shouldn't be there. **Era II hook.**

## 4. The engine of Era II's first arcs
- **The sale-leaseback.** Andes Health Partners wants cash. In 2025 it decides to sell the Caja
  Austral building and lease it back. The 2005 lease gives **Huelén a right of first refusal**.
  Ignacio Larrañaga's lawyers notice; Federico Rehbein's company bids; the fund's lawyers open the
  2005 file and find a lease of "recintos subterráneos" to a family company, and the DOM drawings
  signed by **Carolina Ríos**.
- **The closures.** The fund wants to close Estación Central and Independencia, and sell Plaza
  Italia (with B1 beneath). Whoever buys the Ramón Carnicer wing buys La Capilla.
- **The blackout (25 Feb 2025).** The national grid fails at ≈15:16. It's a test the city fails
  (research/07), and the night someone finds out what's under Toesca.
- **The restoration under La Moneda (2025–26).** Contractors on the far side of the 1990 wall.
- **Javiera's book.**
