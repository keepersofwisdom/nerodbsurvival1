## 1. Orientation: the ground the city stands on

- Santiago lies in a tectonic basin filled with the coalescing **alluvial fans of the Mapocho and Maipo rivers**. Isolated volcanic-rock hills stand out of the gravel: **Santa Lucía** (Huelén), the **San Cristóbal–Tupahue–Chacarillas** massif, **Cerro Blanco** and **Renca** [BK]. Cerro Santa Lucía is promoted as a "basaltic geosite" [REP].
- The colonial grid sits on the Mapocho fan. The **Alameda** (Av. Libertador Bernardo O'Higgins) follows the old dry **southern arm of the Mapocho**, *La Cañada* [BK]. **Plaza Baquedano/Plaza Italia** sits where the river used to fork [DOC — Wikipedia].
- The river was boxed into its present channel from **1888**, between Tobalaba and Estación Mapocho [REP — La Tercera]. Land reclaimed from the riverbed became the **Parque Forestal** [BK].
- Colonial flood walls, the **tajamares** (18th century), survive in fragments under the parks and avenues of the north bank of the Alameda–Providencia axis (§6.3).

**Sources §1:** [Plaza Baquedano – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Baquedano) · [La Tercera – Río Mapocho, testigo natural](https://www.latercera.com/noticia/rio-mapocho-testigo-natural-de-los-472-anos-de-santiago/) · [GuruWalk – Cerro Santa Lucía basaltic geosite](https://guruwalk.com/walks/35232-cerro-santa-lucia-witness-of-the-conquest-of-chile-and-basaltic-geosite)

## 2. La Moneda and the Barrio Cívico

### 2.1 The palace in brief
- A neoclassical palace by **Joaquín Toesca**, built 1784–1805 as the colonial **Casa de Moneda** (Royal Mint). It became the seat of the presidency in the mid-1840s under Manuel Bulnes [BK].
- It stands at the heart of the **Barrio Cívico** (civic district), planned in the 1930s around the Plaza de la Constitución to the north and the Alameda/Plaza Bulnes axis to the south. The plan is associated with the Austrian urbanist Karl Brunner [BK].

### 2.2 Morandé 80 [DOC/REP]
- This is a door on the **east side** of the palace, on calle Morandé. It was built in **1906** under President **Pedro Montt** so that the president could come and go "as a common citizen", without the formal guard salute.
- On **11 September 1973** Salvador Allende's body was carried out through it. During the dictatorship's reconstruction the door was **sealed**. An academic article notes the door was not part of the palace as rebuilt.
- President **Ricardo Lagos** had it rebuilt and reopened on **11 September 2003**, the 30th anniversary of the coup. The work was done by the MOP's Dirección Nacional de Arquitectura. It is opened only on special occasions.

### 2.3 11 September 1973 [BK]
- On the morning of the coup, FACh **Hawker Hunter** jets fired rockets into the palace, around midday. Fire gutted much of the north wing. Allende died inside. The surviving staff were marched out to the street.
- The **Ministry of Defence building facing La Moneda across the Alameda** served as a command node for the coup. A T13 retrospective adds that "**in the palace's basements** existed the first detention and torture centre used by the dictatorship" [REP — T13, 2023]. The wording is ambiguous; it may refer to the Plaza de la Constitución vaults (§2.6).

### 2.4 The reconstruction, 1974–1981 [REP unless marked]
- Work ran from **late 1974 to early 1981**, with an **18-month pause for lack of money**. The total cost has never been published (La Tercera).
- The MOP's **Dirección de Arquitectura** was in charge. The stated aim was a return to Toesca's original concept. The Consejo de Monumentos Nacionales dates completion to 1981.
- The **foundations were reinforced with modern techniques** (EcuRed). The **main offices**, which had faced the Plaza de la Constitución, were **moved to the interior for security**. The **Salón Independencia**, where Allende died, was suppressed and **walled off with concrete** (EcuRed).
- During the works the junta governed from the **Edificio Diego Portales** (§5.1). The palace was **re-inaugurated on 11 March 1981**, the day Pinochet's term under the 1980 Constitution began.
- A testimony quoted by La Tercera says the structure was strengthened during the reconstruction **because a bunker below was already planned** [REP, single testimony].

### 2.5 "El búnker": the underground hall beneath the Plaza de la Constitución
What is solid:
- English Wikipedia says that during the 1973–1980 restorations an **underground office complex, the so-called "bunker", was built under the front square** to give Pinochet a safe exit in case of attack [REP — Wikipedia, no primary source shown].
- **La Tercera** describes the "búnker" as the space Pinochet ordered built **in the early 1980s (1982) beneath the Plaza de la Constitución** "for his most secret activities". It says a hall in the **south-west zone** of the underground had stayed **intact for 30 years**, and that secret meetings of the military government were held there [REP].
- **Emol (Nov 2025)** calls it the "**salón-búnker**", ordered by Pinochet **in 1983 as an amphitheatre**, citing chronicles of the period. Emol also ties the room to the **Informe Rettig** (the 1991 truth-commission report), saying it "was reproduced" there; the exact sense is unclear [REP].
- **Ex-Ante** identifies it as the **Auditorio Prieto**, "known as *el búnker*", built under Pinochet in the 1980s, with **capacity for 150** [REP].
- **Restoration 2025–26** [DOC/REP]: the hall is part of stage I of the MOP's **Plan de Conservación Palacio de La Moneda** (CLP **$4,629 million**, 365 calendar days). The contract went to **Pío V Limitada** for $3,890 million net. The plan includes a new projector and 10 loudspeakers. Works were still running through the **11 March 2026** change of government (Pauta).

**Discrepancy:** construction is dated variously to 1973–80 (Wikipedia), 1982 (La Tercera) and 1983 (Emol). A sensible DM reading: the palace's foundations and sub-levels were prepared during 1974–81, and the auditorium/bunker under the square was fitted out in 1982–83.

**What is *not* documented:** any tunnel from the bunker to another building, its depth, its life-support systems, or any blast rating. All of that is [LEG] or open to the DM.

### 2.6 "El Hoyo": SICAR Cuartel Nº 1 under the Plaza de la Constitución [DOC]
This is the best-documented secret underground space in central Santiago.
- **Location:** in the **subsoil of the Plaza de la Constitución, beside the Teatinos–Agustinas exit of the car park whose vehicle access is at Agustinas 1220**. An official decree text dates the construction to **1935**, which fits the 1930s Barrio Cívico.
- **Before the coup** it housed the Carabineros' **SIAT** (Sección Investigadora de Accidentes de Tránsito, the traffic-accident unit) [REP].
- **In 1974** it was a clandestine **detention and torture site** run by the counter-intelligence staff of the **SICAR** (Servicio de Inteligencia de Carabineros). It was known as **"El Hoyo"** ("the hole").
- **Justice:** Judge **Mario Carroza** indicted **12 former SICAR members** for kidnapping Socialist Party militants held there in 1974. The **Supreme Court confirmed a 10-year sentence** on a retired officer for the kidnapping of **Víctor Zerega Ponce**, who was tortured there and later murdered at Concón. An earlier instance had set 5 years and a day. Another ruling condemned 9 retired Carabineros.
- **Today:** it was opened to the public during the **OH! Stgo** architecture festival (August 2023). A memorial-network coordinator said the space **is used by La Moneda's drivers** (*choferes*).

**DM note:** this establishes three real facts. (1) There is a 1930s vaulted underground under the plaza. (2) Carabineros used it for secret purposes. (3) It sits beside a working underground car park at Agustinas 1220. Treat it as the real anchor of any "La Moneda underground complex" in the fiction.

### 2.7 Plaza de la Ciudadanía and the Centro Cultural La Moneda (CCLM) [DOC/REP]
- On the **south** side of the palace, between it and the Alameda, the **Plaza de la Ciudadanía** was built as a Bicentennial project. Works began in **May 2004** and the plaza opened in **December 2005** (Wikipedia).
- Beneath it lies the **Centro Cultural Palacio La Moneda**, inaugurated on **26 January 2006**. The architects were **Cristián Undurraga and Ana Luisa Devés**. It has **three underground levels, over 7,200 m²**, reached by ramps and paths descending from the plaza. It is the largest legitimately public underground space next to the palace.
- In a 2020 interview, Undurraga said: *"No hay límites para el uso de espacios subterráneos"* ("there are no limits to the use of underground space") [REP — Pauta].
- The **crypt of Bernardo O'Higgins**: after the dictatorship-era **Altar de la Patria** (Plaza Bulnes, with an underground crypt, 1979) was dismantled in the mid-2000s, O'Higgins' remains were moved to a **crypt beneath the Plaza de la Ciudadanía** [BK — verify the year, ~2006].
- **No depth figure** for the CCLM was found.

## 3. Daniela Améstica's *Santiago Subterráneo* and other journalism

### 3.1 The book [DOC — bibliographic]
- **Title:** *Santiago subterráneo. Los túneles, pasadizos y leyendas ocultas bajo la ciudad invisible* ("…the tunnels, passages and hidden legends beneath the invisible city").
- **Author:** Daniela Améstica. Planeta describes her as a researcher of urban history and director of **Viajes Ikigai** (a travel/tour agency); one review calls her a journalist. This is her first book.
- **Publisher and date:** Editorial Planeta (Chile), **7 January 2026**, 160 pp. Print ISBN **9789564088884**; ebook **9789564089003**.
- **Genesis:** the research began as a **tourism project** to design a walking route (Radio Concierto interview).
- **Era note:** the book post-dates Era II's start (2025). In-world, its contents can be "known to cranks and old janitors" well before they are published.

### 3.2 What it claims [REP — as claims by the author]
- **Tunnels linking landmark buildings:** La Moneda, the **Catedral**, the **Portal Fernández Concha** (the arcaded block on the south side of the Plaza de Armas), the **Instituto Nacional** and the **Teatro Municipal**.
- **Basements and passages** under the **ex Congreso Nacional**, the **Hotel Casa Zañartu**, the **Cerro Santa Lucía** and **Barrio República**. Possible forgotten basements in the Portal Fernández Concha that may connect to the Catedral.
- A building **near the Plaza de Armas linked by tunnels spanning entire blocks**. The address **Estado 215** is "surrounded by many mysteries".
- **Scale:** much of the network derives from **old sewer works** and runs **"from Plaza Italia towards San Alberto Hurtado"** (the old Las Rejas axis in Estación Central). Some testimonies claim tunnels reach **Pudahuel and the airport**; the author treats that as **unverified**.
- **Origins proposed:** sewer systems, ventilation systems, the **Jesuit myth**, and **escape routes**. The book "analyses the role of the Jesuit Congregation" and the "secret passages under the ex Congreso".
- **Condition:** most tunnels and accesses are **closed, unsanitary, or at risk of collapse** (Diario Usach).
- **Obstruction:** the **Jesuit community and the National Congress did not answer** her inquiries. "Some answers remain unsaid" (The Clinic).
- **Tone (headlines):** *"Es como que digan que existen los ovni y de repente ves uno"* ("It's as if they tell you UFOs exist and suddenly you see one"; The Clinic, 15 Jan 2026). *"Mucha gente me decía 'estás loca, eso no existe'"* ("Lots of people told me 'you're crazy, that doesn't exist'"; La Cuarta).

### 3.3 On what evidence
- The evidence is **oral testimony**: conserjes (building caretakers), ex-workers, historians, priests and neighbours. It is combined with "archaeological finds" and site visits (publisher's summary).
- It is popular non-fiction in a **testimonial/mystery register**, not an academic study. **No independent confirmation** was found of any specific tunnel *between* the named landmarks.

### 3.4 DM assessment
- Treat the book as **[REP] for "these rumours circulate and someone has stood in some basements"**, and **[LEG] for any specific connection**.
- The underlying claim that **old sewers and services form walkable spaces under the centre is plausible and partly documented** (§4.10, §6.3, §6.4). The interconnections between *named* buildings are not.
- The academic journal *Revista 180* (UDP) makes the key structural point: the **underground city has almost no public records or plans**, and information is **fragmented between companies** (utilities, Metro, concessionaires). That is exactly what lets fiction hide.

## 4. Colonial and 19th-century tunnel legends

**A pattern to know first.** Across Spanish America (Sucre, Cochabamba, Mérida, Antigua Guatemala, Lima) there are legends of tunnels linking churches and convents. When historians and archaeologists investigate, they usually find **crypts and burial vaults, drains, cisterns and cellars**, not inter-building passages. Lima's San Francisco **catacombs are real** (bones of 25,000+ people). Santiago fits this pattern [REP — comparative press].

### 4.2 San Francisco
The 24Horas feature on "myths and realities" of San Francisco quotes the convent's superior, **Fray Manuel Alvarado**: *"túneles y entradas secretas no hay"* ("there are no tunnels or secret entrances"). He conceded that hidden passages **could exist elsewhere** in the capital. The church has survived every great earthquake since 1647 [BK]. Its **crypt** next to the high altar is closed to the public.

### 4.3 The Plaza de Armas block
The Catedral, the Portal Fernández Concha, the Correo Central, the Municipalidad and the Museo Histórico Nacional (Real Audiencia) ring the square [BK]. The **Metro Plaza de Armas** station lies beneath it: Line 5 (2000) and Line 3 (2019), the latter at ~33 m [REP]. Améstica's strongest claim concentrates here: basements under the Portal and a cathedral connection. Any real interconnection would have had to survive the Line 3 excavation of 2015–2018, which disturbed the subsoil deeply.

### 4.4 Cerro Santa Lucía (Huelén)
- Sacred to Indigenous people and a defensive point before and after the Spanish arrival. Pedro de Valdivia founded Santiago at its foot in **February 1541** [BK]. Royalist fortifications (Castillo Hidalgo, 1816–20) [BK]. A US naval astronomical expedition set up an observatory there in the mid-19th century [REP].
- Transformed into a landscaped park from **4 June 1872** by Intendente **Benjamín Vicuña Mackenna**; first stage inaugurated September 1872 [REP]. 2024 marked 150 years of the "cerro-jardín".
- **Why it matters:** it is the only body of **solid volcanic rock** in the old centre. Any "bunker in the rock" story belongs here or on San Cristóbal. Its foot, at **calle Santa Lucía 162**, holds the ex-**Clínica Santa Lucía**, a DINA clinic and torture site (§5.2).

### 4.5 Cerro San Cristóbal (Parque Metropolitano)
- The Túnel San Cristóbal runs under **Cerro Tupahue**, part of the Parque Metropolitano; popular usage calls the whole park "San Cristóbal" [DOC — MOP].
- The park holds the funicular (1925), the cable car, the Virgin statue (1908), the zoo, and public swimming pools (Tupahue, Antilén) [BK]. Water-supply installations are plausible but were not verified.

### 4.6 Cerro Blanco
A small hill in Recoleta near the Cementerio General and the Line 2 station of the same name. Historically quarried, and it holds pre-Hispanic cup-mark stones [BK]. No verified tunnels.

### 4.7 Jesuit tunnels
The "Jesuit tunnel" is a pan-Hispanic trope: the Jesuits were rich, secretive and expelled in 1767, so they must have hidden things underground. In Santiago it attaches to the Compañía church site (ex Congreso gardens) and to the Jesuits' colonial properties. Améstica says the Jesuits would not answer her. **[LEG].** It is the perfect "the old men knew" myth.

### 4.8 The 1905–1910 sewers as a source of legend
Much of what witnesses call "tunnels" is probably the **brick and stone collector system** built for the Centenary (§6.3). It was laid under the **north–south streets** with smaller east–west laterals, giving a characteristic zig-zag network. It was built by a French contractor using **Parisian methods** [DOC]. Large vaulted sections are walkable.

### 4.9 Documented: the La Chimba hospital tunnel (Independencia/Recoleta) [DOC]
- **Found:** July 2019, during **Enel** (power utility) works on **Av. La Paz**, opposite the Facultad de Odontología. Announced on **4 July 2019** by Independencia mayor **Gonzalo Durán**, with the dean, Irene Morales.
- **Connects:** the **Instituto Psiquiátrico Dr. José Horwitz Barak** (the former **Casa de Orates**, the 19th-century lunatic asylum; Recoleta side) with the **Facultad de Odontología, Universidad de Chile** (Independencia side), beneath Av. La Paz.
- **Construction:** stone and brick. 2019 press said ~10 m long. **El Mostrador (2021)**, citing the *Memorias de la Casa de Orates*, gives **31.5 m long, 2.20 m wide, 2.3 m high**, **begun 1905 and finished 1906**, linking the women's *pensionado* (paying ward) with the **Pabellón Barros**. Meganoticias dates it to c. 1850. The discrepancy is unresolved.
- Emol's headline calls it "part of an **old underground network of hospital infrastructure**". The mayor asked the **Consejo de Monumentos Nacionales** to locate **all the tunnels of this network** and floated an underground "**museo de la salud**" (health museum). No published outcome was found.
- **DM significance:** an officially acknowledged **hospital tunnel network** in the Independencia/Recoleta hospital cluster, which is also home to the Hospital Clínico U. de Chile, Hospital San José, Roberto del Río, the Instituto Nacional del Cáncer, the Servicio Médico Legal and, a few blocks west, **Cuartel Borgoño** (§5.2).

### 4.10 Documented: the Barrio Yungay sewer vault [DOC]
- At **Erasmo Escala 2767** (Santiago, west), works for a **CESFAM** (Centro de Salud Familiar, a public primary-care clinic) began in **December 2015** and uncovered **"a barrel vault from the early 20th century, part of Santiago's old sewer system"**, crossed by water ducts.
- Works **stopped 2016–2017** for a conservation project. Construction later stalled with the contractor and was **reactivated in 2020**.
- The vault is to be **visible under a glass floor at the CESFAM entrance**. Opening was expected around **March 2026**, not confirmed.
- **DM significance:** Chilean law forces works to stop when heritage appears underground. A health building ended up literally built over a historic tunnel.

## 5. Dictatorship-era underground spaces (1973–1990)

### 5.1 Edificio Diego Portales (now GAM, Centro Cultural Gabriela Mistral), Alameda 227
- Built 1971–72 for the UN **UNCTAD III** conference [BK]. Under Allende it became the Centro Cultural Gabriela Mistral.
- After the coup it was **closed to the public and occupied by the junta as the seat of government**, renamed Edificio Diego Portales. It was "**armoured, fenced, and its art collection dismantled**" [DOC — GAM]. Pinochet's office door was **lined with steel plates on both sides** [REP — DF].
- After the executive returned to La Moneda in 1981, the building remained the **legislative seat** (the junta's legislative commissions). The tower later housed the **Ministry of Defence** [DOC/REP].
- **A fire in early 2006 destroyed ~40%** of the building [DOC]. Bachelet's first government decided to restore its cultural purpose, and **GAM opened in 2010** [DOC].
- **Bunker:** none found in any source [REP-negative]. "Pinochet's bunker under Diego Portales" is **[LEG]**. The building is ~1.5 km east of La Moneda, two blocks from **Plaza Italia**, and directly over **Metro Line 1** (Universidad Católica–Baquedano).

### 5.4 Hospital Militar (old site)
- The **old Hospital Militar** in Providencia moved to **La Reina in 2009**. The Army handed the building to MINSAL, and it became the **Hospital Metropolitano**, at **Holanda 060, Providencia** [DOC]. Pinochet died at the Hospital Militar on 10 Dec 2006, i.e. at the old Providencia building [BK].
- 2009: 200 beds (36 critical). A 2011 agreement extended MINSAL's use for 15 years (to ~2026). The building **reopened 15 May 2020 as a COVID hospital** (300 beds planned, 46 ventilated) [DOC].
- **2021:** critical beds cut from 122 to 24 because of **construction of Costanera Center car parks** next door [REP]. Possibly declared a National Monument in Oct 2022 (incomplete source) [REP].
- **DM significance:** an ex-military hospital whose MINSAL lease expires around **2026**, adjacent to Santiago's deepest commercial basements.

### 5.5 Civil-defence shelters
- **No evidence was found of a Cold-War public shelter programme in Santiago** [REP-negative].
- During the **Beagle crisis** (most tense **12–22 Dec 1978**; defused by papal mediation) Argentina ran blackout drills (e.g. Neuquén, 14 Dec 1978), and Chile made "similar preparations" in the south. No shelter construction in the capital is documented.
- Chile's civil-protection agency was ONEMI, replaced by SENAPRED in 2023 [BK].
- **Implication:** in-world, there are **no public bunkers** to fall back on. Private ones would be unique.

### 5.6 A real precedent for a hidden bunker
- **Colonia Dignidad** (Maule region): searches under the national search plan for the disappeared turned up a **secret bunker in December 2024** [REP — headline]. The German enclave's buried arms caches and hidden structures are notorious [BK]. Not in Santiago, but it shows Chileans that "secret bunkers built by powerful old men" are not fantasy.

## 6. Infrastructure tunnels and voids

### 6.1 Metro de Santiago

**Construction history**
- **Line 1:** works began **29 May 1969** at Las Rejas/Alameda, following a 1968 transport-plan decree and studies by the French–Chilean consortium BCEOM–SOFRETU–CADE. The first section, **San Pablo–La Moneda**, opened on **15 September 1975**, inaugurated by Pinochet: ~8.4 km, 12 stations, mostly underground **under the Alameda**, built **cut-and-cover ("a tajo abierto")**, which tore up the avenue for years [DOC/REP].
- Line 1 extensions: to **Salvador** (1977, through Universidad de Chile, Santa Lucía, Universidad Católica and Baquedano), to **Escuela Militar** (1980), and to **Los Dominicos** (2010) [BK].
- **The 1977 Salvador works uncovered the tajamares** (§6.3) [REP].
- Line 2 (1978 onward); Line 5 (1997 Baquedano–La Florida; extended west through Bellas Artes and Plaza de Armas in 2000, and to Maipú in 2010–11); Line 4 and 4A (2005–06); Line 6 (Nov 2017); Line 3 (Jan 2019, ~17 km, five comunas; Quilicura extension 3.8 km, three stations, ~US$379 M, 2023) [BK for dates except where cited; Line 3 data REP].
- **Line 7** (Renca–Vitacura) is under construction, expected **~2028**. Its **Vespucio/Alonso de Córdova** station will be **~45 m deep (some say 50 m) and served only by lifts** [REP].

**Depths**
- Line 1 is shallow cut-and-cover; its exact depth was not verified [EST ~8–15 m to rail].
- Lines 3 and 6 are mined (NATM, sprayed-concrete) tunnels and deep stations. **Plaza de Armas and Puente Cal y Canto (Line 3) are ~33 m deep**, the deepest operating stations as of the late 2010s [REP]. Line 6 platforms were "much commented on" for their depth; no figure was found.
- The tunnelling between **Puente Cal y Canto and Plaza de Armas** broke through in **July 2016** [REP].

**Ghost stations and unused works** [REP]
- **Libertad (a.k.a. Yungay)**, Line 5, at **Catedral with Libertad**, a block from Plaza Yungay. The **structure is complete, including accesses and platforms**, but it was never opened: Metro cites projected demand. It has been closed for over two decades. Metro handed the surface **Plaza Libertad** to the community in Dec 2013 as part of the Line 5 extension.
- BioBioChile (2014) classifies ghost stations as (a) built partly or fully but unused, (b) projected and cancelled, and (c) pure rumour.
- A Jan 2019 BioBioChile article (cited on Wikipedia's "**Echeverría** metro station" page) concerns a **station built some 30 years earlier that would not be used**. The details were **not verified** this session.

**Service spaces** [BK/EST]: ventilation shafts, emergency exits, cable galleries, pump sumps, and construction shafts (*piques*) left as ventilation or access points. Every mined line was built from shafts sunk in plazas and lots, which later became fan shafts or exits. These are the most plausible "unlabelled doors" under the city.

**2019:** on **18 October 2019** the estallido social began with mass fare-evasion protests in Metro. Many stations were burned or damaged that night and after; Metro reported over a hundred damaged and around two dozen burned [BK — verify exact counts]. **Baquedano** was closed for long periods. In October 2019 there were **allegations that a room in Baquedano station was used to hold and mistreat detainees**. These remain contested and were not verified here.

### 6.3 Water underground: the Mapocho, tajamares, canals and collectors
- **Tajamares** (colonial flood walls). A **brick wall in good condition** was found **in 1977 under Parque Balmaceda during the Line 1 Salvador works**. Architects kept it and built a **sunken plaza and a room showing a 30 m section** (the **Museo de los Tajamares**, a National Monument). It became unusable in 2003. **After the 2019 unrest it was buried again**, and at least three rescue attempts have failed. Other remains have been recorded along the **Parque Forestal**, **Av. Providencia opposite the Seminario**, and at **Miguel Claro** (Edificio Véneto excavation). [REP/DOC]
- **Canal San Carlos.** Takes water from the **Maipo** to the **Mapocho**: **31.5 km**, discharging into the Mapocho **opposite Costanera Center**. It crosses Puente Alto, La Florida, Peñalolén, La Reina, Providencia and Las Condes. Its operating society was formed on **5 July 1827** and the canal was **finished in 1829** (bicentenary 2027). It is **eastern Santiago's main storm-water collector**. [REP] Covered or vaulted reaches were not verified; it is mostly an open, fast, dangerous channel [BK].
- **Zanjón de la Aguada.** A natural storm channel south of the centre that became a receptor of sewage. It overflowed repeatedly in the 20th century (e.g. 1920; also 2001), and was **piped and canalised from the 1990s** [REP].
- **The 1905–1910 sewer system ("alcantarillado del Centenario").** The 1903 Ley de Alcantarillado y Pavimentación and the 1901 definitive project led to **Supreme Decree 5,005 of 27 Dec 1904** and a contract signed in Jan 1905 with the **Société de Construction des Batignolles** (through Fould y Cía.). Construction ran **1905/1907–1910**, using **Parisian methods**. Main collectors run under the **north–south streets** (steeper gradient) and secondary conduits east–west. There are two basins, north and south of the Mapocho; the southern one originally drained by open channels to the Zanjón de la Aguada. Materials were **brick, cement, lime, stone and sand** for collectors, outfalls and **vaults (bóvedas)**. [DOC/REP]
- Today the sewers belong to **Aguas Andinas** (ex-EMOS, privatised 1999) [BK]. Large collectors and interceptors were added during the Mapocho clean-up of the 2000s–2010s [BK].
- **Plaza Italia/Baquedano** sits over the old river fork. The "Mapocho Pedaleable" cycle path in the riverbed is a recurring project [REP].

### 6.4 The San Borja tunnels: a real, walkable network under the centre [DOC/REP]
This is **the single most useful real fact for the campaign.**
- The **Remodelación San Borja** is a modernist tower estate in central Santiago, begun in **1969 under President Eduardo Frei Montalva** (CORMU, the state urban-renewal corporation) [REP]. It sits on the old Hospital San Francisco de Borja lands, between the Alameda and Av. Portugal–Marcoleta, **next to the Posta Central (HUAP) and the Hospital Clínico UC**, a few blocks south-west of Plaza Italia [BK — check the street boundaries].
- When the builders **hit groundwater (napas)**, they built a **water-capture plant, a thermal (heating) plant and a distribution network** [REP].
- That network runs through **walkable concrete tunnels of about 4 km** carrying **more than 12 km of pipes** (Fundación Aldea; AmoSantiago). A COSSBO executive speaks of **7.5 km of tunnels** (Conferencia Internacional de Ciudad). The discrepancy is unresolved; perhaps 4 km of main galleries plus branches.
- It still distributes **drinking water, hot water and heating** to the towers. It is run by **COSSBO** (Comunidad de Servicios Remodelación San Borja), which has a 2022–2036 development plan.
- **About 50 towers were planned and only 21 built**: 18 residential, 3 institutional.
- **Public access:** night walks by Fundación Aldea; **guided tours in August 2026** tied to the Cámara Chilena de la Construcción's **CIC 2026** conference. Registrations filled at once.
- **DM significance:** a **private, self-sufficient water-and-heat utility with its own wells and boiler plant, in walkable tunnels**, run by a residents' services community, **next to the country's busiest emergency hospital**. It is a real-world template for a hidden lifeline.

### 6.5 Car parks, vaults, galleries
- **Underground car parks.** Plaza de la Constitución (vehicle access **Agustinas 1220**) [DOC]. Modern towers in Providencia/Las Condes typically have 4–8 basement levels [BK/EST]. Municipal concession car parks exist under several plazas and avenues; individual sites were not verified.
- **Vaults.** The **Banco Central** (open on Día de los Patrimonios with registration) [REP]. The **Registro Civil vault** shown in 2026 [REP]. The **Bolsa de Comercio** (1917 building, calle La Bolsa) and old bank headquarters on Huérfanos/Bandera/Ahumada have strongrooms [BK].
- **Galerías and caracoles.** The centre's covered shopping arcades and 1970s–80s spiral malls (*caracoles*) have **basement levels**, many semi-abandoned, plus service corridors that cross under blocks [BK]. Améstica's "block-spanning tunnels" may partly be these.

### 6.6 Hospitals: basements, galleries, tunnels
- **Hospital del Salvador** (Providencia; founded **7 Dec 1871**). On **23 May 2026** a 44-year-old psychiatric patient escaped **through the ducts of his room's bathroom into the hospital's underground**. A fire captain described **"pozos y canales subterráneos"** (underground shafts and channels). The hospital said its **"complex structure, with old tunnels and passages"** hampered the search. He reappeared hours later, unhurt; versions differ on whether he returned by himself. [REP]
- **New Hospital del Salvador + Instituto Nacional de Geriatría.** Concession awarded 2014; works began ~2017; it was due in 2019 and is called the most delayed concession in Chilean history. **641 beds, 26 operating theatres (2 angiography), ~US$400 M, ~172,000 m²**. It was **98.5% complete in May 2026**, with partial operation planned for spring 2026. The old pavilion hospital, and its tunnels, will be **vacated** [REP].
- **Casa de Orates/Horwitz tunnel** (§4.9): an acknowledged "network of hospital infrastructure" in La Chimba [DOC].
- **General pattern** [BK/EST]: 19th- and early-20th-century **pavilion hospitals** (Salvador, San José, San Juan de Dios, the old San Borja, the Casa de Orates) were built as separate wards linked by **covered or underground service galleries** for steam heating, laundry carts, food and corpses. Modern hospitals have **deep basements** for imaging, morgues, generators, fuel and water reserves. **Radiotherapy rooms are literally called "búnkers"** in Chilean hospital jargon: thick concrete shielded vaults that are legal, inspected, and expected to be massive.
- **Not found:** any documented tunnel between the Posta Central and the San Borja tunnels or the Hospital Clínico UC, or between the Hospital del Trabajador and anything. Those are open to the DM.

## 7. Geology and engineering: what digging deep really means

### 7.1 The "grava de Santiago" [DOC]
- The east and centre of the city stand on the **Santiago Gravel**, the coarse fluvial deposits of the Mapocho (and the Maipo to the south): sandy gravels with cobbles and boulders (*bolones*), dense and slightly cemented.
- Engineering papers call it a soil of **"alta competencia geotécnica"**: it stands steeply in cuts and supports deep excavations with modest shoring.
- **Typical deep excavations in the literature:**
  - **21.2 m in Las Condes**, retained with machine-bored piles and **two rows of temporary ground anchors**;
  - **28 m for the Beauchef Poniente building** (U. de Chile, Santiago Centro), in Mapocho fluvial deposits.
- The north and west (Pudahuel, Quilicura, parts of Maipú and Renca) have **finer soils and volcanic ash** (the "cenizas de Pudahuel"), which are weaker and, in places, wetter [BK].

### 7.2 Groundwater [DOC/REP]
- The water table in the Santiago gravel is generally **below 50 m**.
- A U. de Chile field-visit note (2011) gives **40–50 m or more at the Costa Azul building (Providencia) and Eurocentro (Santiago Centro)**, and **25–30 m at the ISE building (Las Condes)**. Treat these as indicative; local conditions vary.
- **Exceptions:** **perched water (napas colgadas)** and old canal or acequia seepage. Near the **Mapocho and Canal San Carlos**, water rises. The **San Borja** builders "hit groundwater" in 1969–70 and turned it into a well field [REP]. Line 3 has had **drainage problems** (2024) [REP].
- **Implication:** a bunker in the centre or Providencia at **20–35 m is dry by default**. Below ~40 m you approach the aquifer, which is a hazard to excavation but a **resource** (a private well) for a survivalist.

### 7.3 Rock
- The **Santa Lucía** and **San Cristóbal/Tupahue** hills are volcanic rock (Santa Lucía is promoted as basaltic) [REP]. Rock tunnels there would be **self-supporting but slow, noisy and blast-dependent**, and so very hard to hide. The **Túnel San Cristóbal** (2008) shows it can be done at scale [DOC].

### 7.4 Seismicity
- **Subduction megathrusts:** 3 March 1985 (Mw ~8.0, central coast) and **27 Feb 2010 (Mw 8.8, Maule)** shook Santiago hard [BK]. Damage in 2010 tracked **soil conditions**: worst on fine soils and fills, mildest on the gravel [REP — ResearchGate study title].
- **Falla San Ramón** (crustal fault at the foot of the Andes) [REP]:
  - It is **active**, running ~**30–50 km** along the eastern piedmont through **Vitacura, Las Condes, La Reina, Peñalolén, (Macul), La Florida and Puente Alto**, possibly continuing to Lo Barnechea and Pirque.
  - Worst case is **Mw ~7.4–7.5**, with a **recurrence of thousands of years**. Two dated ruptures: **~17,000 and ~8,000 years ago**.
  - A Chamber of Deputies study ranks **Puente Alto (61.8% of population exposed)** and **Las Condes (55.4%)** most at risk.
  - Its trace has been debated in **zoning (planes reguladores)**.
- **Underground structures and earthquakes** [BK/EST]: buried boxes move *with* the ground and generally fare better than tall buildings. Santiago's Metro tunnels came through 2010 essentially intact. **The danger is at portals, shafts, joints with buildings, and anything crossing a fault**. A bunker **straddling the San Ramón trace** could be sheared. Design is normally governed by soil–structure interaction under Chilean seismic standards (NCh433 for buildings, plus Metro's own criteria) [BK].

### 7.5 Practical depth limits (for the DM) [EST]

| Depth | Feasibility in the Santiago gravel (centre/Providencia) | Hiding difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 m | Trivial: one or two basement levels | Low; any building has them |
| 10–25 m | Routine: 4–8 parking levels with piles and anchors | Medium. The spoil volume is large but normal for a tower. |
| 25–35 m | Done (Metro L3 stations ~33 m; 28 m building excavations) | High: needs serious shoring, dewatering contingency and monitoring |
| 35–50 m | Exceptional (L7 deep station ~45 m); approaches the water table | Very high; specialist contractors, noticed by the trade |
| >50 m | Into the aquifer: pumping and waterproofing | Effectively impossible to hide |

## 8. Rules: who regulates digging, and how hard it is to hide

### 8.1 The legal stack [BK — verify article numbers before quoting in-game]

| **PRMS**, Plan Regulador Metropolitano de Santiago (1994) | Metropolitan zoning, risk areas, the urban limit |
| **PRC**, Plan Regulador Comunal (each comuna) | Heights, densities, uses, heritage zones (Zonas Típicas, Inmuebles de Conservación Histórica). Providencia's and Santiago's PRCs differ a lot. |
| **DOM**, Dirección de Obras Municipales (each municipality) | Issues the **permiso de edificación** (building permit, covering excavation and basements), demolition permits and the **recepción final** (occupancy sign-off). An independent **revisor** may certify compliance, and large buildings need an independent **structural reviewer**. |
| **Ley 17.288** de Monumentos Nacionales (1970) | Any archaeological find is a national monument by law. **Works must stop and notify the CMN** (Consejo de Monumentos Nacionales). See the Erasmo Escala vault, which stopped works for two years [DOC]. |
| **Ley 19.300** (environment) / SEIA | Large projects in Santiago (a saturated air-quality zone) may need environmental review |
| **Ley 20.958** (aportes al espacio público) | Mobility impact mitigation (IMIV) for larger projects |
| **Metro de Santiago** | In practice the DOM requires Metro's technical clearance for excavations adjacent to its tunnels and stations; Metro holds easements (*servidumbres*) over land above its works. **The exact legal instrument was not verified.** |
| **Public subsoil** (under streets and plazas, *bienes nacionales de uso público*) | Requires a municipal/SERVIU concession or permit; this is how underground car parks under plazas are done |
| **SEREMI de Salud / Superintendencia de Salud** | Sanitary authorisation and accreditation of clinics. Radiotherapy vaults need radiological licensing. |
| **Utilities** (Aguas Andinas, Enel, Metrogas, telecoms) | Their networks must be located (*catastro*) and protected; they discover things (cf. Enel finding the La Paz tunnel) |

### 8.2 How a legal deep basement happens [BK/EST]
1. Buy the lot and get a **Certificado de Informaciones Previas** (zoning certificate) from the DOM.
2. Design and file for the **permiso de edificación**, with soil-mechanics report, shoring design (piles + anchors), structural calculations and neighbour protections. Anchors that pass under neighbouring lots need their consent.
3. Notify Metro and the utilities if nearby. Tower projects may also trigger SEIA/IMIV.
4. Excavate and shore (*entibación*): bored piles, then anchors level by level. Inspections follow, plus the archaeological watch (by law, any find stops work).
5. **Recepción final** by the DOM before occupancy. Plans are archived at the DOM and are **public records**, though rarely consulted.

### 8.3 Spoil: the tell-tale [EST]
| Example | In-situ volume | Loose volume (×1.3–1.4) | Truckloads (10–14 m³) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standalone secret tunnel, 2.5 × 2.5 m × 500 m | ~3,100 m³ | ~4,100–4,400 m³ | **~300–440** |
| A bunker box 30 × 15 × 5 m, plus access shaft | ~2,500 m³ | ~3,300–3,500 m³ | ~240–350 |
| A whole 40 × 30 m lot dug to 20 m (6 parking levels) | ~24,000 m³ | ~31,000–34,000 m³ | **~2,200–3,400** |

- **Lesson:** a secret bunker is easiest to hide **inside the excavation of a legal deep basement**. The extra 10–15% of spoil is lost in the noise of a tower project. A standalone tunnel produces hundreds of unexplained truck movements, which neighbours, the DOM and air-quality inspectors notice.
- **Disposal:** spoil must go to authorised fills or *botaderos*. Illegal dumping on the city's edge is common and is itself a clue. Trucks need covered loads, wheel-washing and set hours. Noise is limited by municipal ordinances and the national noise standard. Winter air-quality episodes (pre-emergencies) can halt earthworks.
- **Workforce:** shoring, anchoring and mining crews come from a small pool of specialist firms that also work for Metro and the highway concessions. **They talk.**

### 8.4 Covers that make deep, thick concrete normal [EST/BK]
- **Hospital radiotherapy bunker:** a legally required 1.5–2.5 m concrete shielded room for a linear accelerator, called a "búnker".
- **Imaging suites** (MRI, PET-CT), **morgues**, **pharmacy vaults**.
- **Emergency generator rooms, fuel tanks and water reserves**, which clinics must have. Water storage tanks of hundreds of m³ are common.
- **Data centres and server rooms**; **bank strongrooms**; **cold-storage** units.
- **Multi-level car parks**; **utility galleries**.
- **Seismic isolation basements** (base-isolated hospitals and clinics have a full crawl level of isolators) [BK].
- The real San Borja precedent: a **private district-heating and well-water utility** in tunnels (§6.4).

## 9. Hooks for play (FICTION IDEAS, not facts)

*Everything in this section is invented for the game. Each hook names the real anchor that makes it plausible.*

1. **The San Borja lifeline.** *(Anchor: the COSSBO tunnels, §6.4.)* The founder buys a small building on the edge of the San Borja estate for his first stabilisation unit. His basement shares a wall with a COSSBO gallery. Over the years he quietly buys COSSBO goodwill, and a door. He gets well water, a boiler plant, and 4–7.5 km of walkable concrete running past the back of the **Posta Central** and the **Hospital Clínico UC**.
2. **The radiotherapy bunker that never got a linac.** *(Anchor: legal "búnker de radioterapia", §8.4.)* A Providencia clinic files for a radiotherapy room in 2007. The 2-m-walled vault is built and inspected, then "the project is postponed". The vault is real; its lower level is not on file.
3. **The seventh parking level.** *(Anchor: routine 20–28 m excavations, §7.)* An office tower near **Plaza Italia/Ramón Carnicer** is permitted with six basement levels and dug to eight. The DOM's archived plans show six. The concrete contractor's invoices show eight.
4. **The Hospital del Trabajador block.** *(Anchor: a deep 2025 ER on floor −1; Metro L1/L5 at Baquedano a block away.)* The old men's cabal funded a "seismic retrofit" next door in the 1990s. The bunker sits between the hospital's basement and the Line 5 tunnel, reached through a Metro emergency-exit shaft that was "decommissioned" in 1997.
5. **Auditorio Prieto's third door.** *(Anchor: the bunker and El Hoyo under the Plaza de la Constitución, §2.5–2.6.)* During the 2025–26 restoration a contractor from Pío V finds a sealed doorway in the south-west hall. It leads to a 1935 vault, the drivers' rooms, and something poured in 1982 that is on no plan. He calls the doctor because the doctor once saved his son.
6. **The Salvador ducts.** *(Anchor: the May 2026 escape, §6.6.)* When the old Hospital del Salvador empties into its new building, its pavilion tunnels lie abandoned under Providencia, a few hundred metres from the Tórax, the Asenjo and the Calvo Mackenna. Era II's heir hears from a psychiatric patient where the tunnels go.
7. **The La Chimba network.** *(Anchor: the 1905–06 Casa de Orates tunnel, and the mayor's call to map "all the tunnels of this network", §4.9.)* The CMN survey was never published because one tunnel ran toward **Cuartel Borgoño**. The old men know why.
8. **The doctors of Santa Lucía 162.** *(Anchor: DINA's Clínica Santa Lucía, §5.2.)* One of the bunker-building old men trained as a physician in 1974 and once worked at a "clinic at the foot of the hill". His bunker beneath a neighbouring building **on the other side of the street** was his penance, or his insurance. *(Keep the real memorial site as a memorial; invent the neighbour.)*
9. **The Libertad ghost station.** *(Anchor: the finished but never-opened Line 5 station, §6.1.)* Access to a sealed platform is possible through a Barrio Yungay building's boiler room. Metro inspects twice a year. The founder stores ambulances' worth of supplies there, until Metro announces it will finally open the station.
10. **The 1974 rebuild.** *(Anchor: the Presidente Errázuriz house rebuilt after Prats' murder, §5.3.)* A retired engineer's memoir hints that the rebuilt residence "had more below than above". It is the template the rich old men copied in the 1980s.
11. **Under the riverbed.** *(Anchor: the Costanera Norte under-river tunnel and the April 2016 flood, §6.2.)* The heir's network has an emergency link to a concession maintenance gallery. When the Mapocho rises, as it did in 2016, the link floods first.
12. **The gravel's mercy and the fault's threat.** *(Anchor: dry gravel to 40–50 m; the San Ramón fault, §7.)* The central bunkers are dry and stable. The cabal's eastern refuge in La Reina was built across the San Ramón trace "because the land was cheap". Era III's quake shears its access tunnel.
13. **The well.** *(Anchor: the San Borja wells; water table ~40–50 m.)* The doctor drills a "fire-fighting reserve" borehole that is actually a potable well into the aquifer. In the apocalypse, water is the real currency.
14. **Six hundred trucks.** *(Anchor: spoil arithmetic, §8.3.)* A journalist (an Améstica-like figure, 2026) cross-references DOM permits with truck manifests and finds a building that removed twice the soil its basement needs. She calls the clinic for comment.
15. **The Portal Fernández Concha basements.** *(Anchor: Améstica's claims, §3.2.)* The DM may decide some of her "testimonies" are true. A conserje's grandfather walled up a passage from the Portal toward the Catedral crypt in 1960; the old men reopened it in 1988.
16. **The Line 7 cover.** *(Anchor: Line 7's 45-m Vitacura station and its construction shafts, 2022–2028, §6.1.)* Metro's own shafts give a once-in-a-generation cover for deep excavation in Vitacura and Providencia. Bribes in the contractor's logistics let a private drift be dug toward a clinic's basement.

Era II opens with the building's future in doubt.
18. **The Centenary sewers.** *(Anchor: the 1905–1910 French-built brick collectors under north–south streets, §6.3.)* An old Aguas Andinas inspector sells the doctor a hand-drawn map of walkable ovoid collectors from **Portugal to Plaza Italia**. Half of them were bricked up in the 1990s; the map doesn't say which half.
19. **No public shelters.** *(Anchor: no civil-defence shelter programme was ever built, §5.5.)* When Era III comes, Santiago has nowhere official to hide. Rumours of "the old men's bunkers" become a political fact, and a mob's target.

Providencia, buried again after 2019, §6.3.)* A unit in Providencia hits an 18th-century brick tajamar during excavation. By law the work must stop and the CMN must be told.
