### 1.4 The mental map
- **"De Plaza Italia para arriba / para abajo"** ("from Plaza Italia up / down") is the city's class line [BK]. *Arriba* (east and up the slope toward the Andes) means Providencia, Las Condes, Vitacura, Lo Barnechea and La Reina: richer, whiter, private healthcare. *Abajo* means the rest.
- **The axis** is one continuous avenue: the Alameda (Santiago) becomes Av. Providencia at Plaza Italia, then Av. Apoquindo in Las Condes, with Metro Line 1 beneath it the whole way [BK].
- **The river** (Mapocho) runs east–west, canalised. North of it lie La Chimba (Recoleta/Independencia), Bellavista and the cerro San Cristóbal.
- **The smog** is a winter (May–August) phenomenon: pre-emergency and emergency days, driving restrictions, the brown layer over the city visible from the cerros [BK].

## 3. District entries

### 3.1 Santiago Centro: the civic district (La Moneda, Plaza de Armas, Paseo Ahumada)
- **Where:** comuna de Santiago. The old Spanish grid between the Mapocho (north), the Alameda (south), Cerro Santa Lucía (east) and the Norte–Sur motorway trench (west).
- **Character:** government, courts, banks, wholesale and retail commerce, cheap lunch counters, pedestrian *paseos*. Since the 1990s it has also gained residential towers (municipal *repoblamiento* policy) [BK]. Busy by day, half-empty and edgy by night.
- **Landmarks:** La Moneda and the Barrio Cívico (Plaza de la Constitución, Plaza de la Ciudadanía, Centro Cultural La Moneda) [DOC]. **Plaza de Armas**: the Catedral, the Correo Central, the Museo Histórico Nacional (ex-Real Audiencia), the Municipalidad, and the Portal Fernández Concha with its cheap *completo* and *empanada* counters [BK]. **Paseo Ahumada** and **Paseo Huérfanos** (pedestrian streets) [BK]. The Bolsa de Comercio, the Club de la Unión, the ex Congreso Nacional and the Tribunales de Justicia. The Teatro Municipal, the Biblioteca Nacional, the Iglesia de San Francisco, Barrio París-Londres (and **Londres 38**, the DINA memorial). The **Mercado Central** and **Estación Mapocho** on the river. The Torre Entel (telecoms tower) [BK].
- **Metro:** La Moneda (L1, 1975), Universidad de Chile (L1 1977/L3 2019), Plaza de Armas (L5 2000/L3 2019), Santa Lucía (L1 1977), Puente Cal y Canto (L2 1987/L3 2019), Los Héroes (L1/L2).
- **2005:** Lagos' last year. **La Moneda's courtyards opened for pedestrians to cross** [BK]. The **Plaza de la Ciudadanía** opened in Dec 2005 and the **Centro Cultural La Moneda** on 26 Jan 2006 [DOC]. **Yellow micros** jam every street (Transantiago is still two years off). Paseo Ahumada has its **"cafés con piernas"** (coffee bars with short-skirted waitresses), shoeshiners, street preachers, money changers on Agustinas, and ambulantes scattering from Carabineros. Office life still centres here, but the banks are drifting east to Sanhattan [BK].
- **~2019:** the **Alameda is the protest corridor** from 18 October. Shopfronts are welded over with steel sheets, every façade carries graffiti, and burned bus shelters, barricades and tear gas drift down from Plaza Italia. La Moneda is ringed by fences and Carabineros. Weekday commerce collapses; many small businesses never reopen [BK].
- **2025:** post-pandemic office vacancy. **Massive street vending (comercio ambulante)** on Ahumada, Estado and Meiggs, and a strong perception of insecurity. Mayor Desbordes runs a security/"order" agenda. **La Moneda is under restoration** (MOP conservation plan, 2025–26, including the underground "búnker"/Auditorio Prieto) [DOC]. The Peruvian, Venezuelan, Haitian and Colombian presence is visible everywhere. The **25 Feb 2025 blackout** stranded commuters underground [BK].
- **Game notes:** this is where the real underground lies (see file 03: the Plaza de la Constitución vaults, the 1905–1910 collectors, Améstica's Portal Fernández Concha rumours). Any clinic here gets **trauma from street crime, falls, syncope among elderly shoppers, overdoses and psychiatric crises**.

### 3.2 Barrio Lastarria, Bellas Artes and the Parque Forestal
- **Where:** between Cerro Santa Lucía and Plaza Italia, from the Alameda north to the Mapocho, in comuna de Santiago.
- **Character:** the city's bohemian-intellectual quarter. Early-20th-century apartment buildings, small theatres and cinemas, bookshops, restaurants, hostels. Gentrified, touristic and young.
- **Landmarks:** calle José Victorino Lastarria with the Plaza Mulato Gil de Castro and the Museo de Artes Visuales (MAVI). The **GAM** (Centro Cultural Gabriela Mistral, Alameda 227; ex-Edificio Diego Portales, the junta's HQ; reopened 2010) [DOC]. The **Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes** (1910) with the MAC behind it. The **Parque Forestal**, laid out on land reclaimed from the riverbed, with the Fuente Alemana (German fountain) [BK]. The UC Casa Central on the Alameda [BK]. **Calle Belgrado 11** (reportedly the DINA's HQ) [BK — verify].
- **Metro:** Universidad Católica (L1 1977), Bellas Artes (L5 2000), Baquedano (L1/L5), Santa Lucía (L1).
- **2005:** an emerging "it" barrio: Cine El Biógrafo, small *picadas* (cheap eateries), artists. The Diego Portales tower is still Defence offices; the convention block is closed and grim. Fire in early 2006 [DOC].
- **~2019:** **on the front line.** Daily tear gas and water cannon (*guanaco*) along the park, and running fights on the riverbank. Restaurants close for months. GAM becomes an informal refuge and assembly hall [BK].
- **2025:** partial recovery. Tourism has returned, but it is grittier than in 2015. GAM is an established cultural hub (15 years).
- **Game notes:** a natural location for a **"unit" or a Founder's apartment** in 2005: central, a walk to the Posta Central and Plaza Italia, and atop Metro L1.

### 3.3 Cerro Santa Lucía (Huelén)
- **Where:** a rock hill between the Alameda, Santa Lucía street, Merced and Victoria Subercaseaux. Comuna de Santiago.
- **Character:** a 19th-century romantic park (Vicuña Mackenna, 1872) [REP] with stairs, terraces and fountains. The city's founding site (1541) [BK]. Gates close at night [BK].
- **Landmarks:** Terraza Neptuno, Castillo Hidalgo, Vicuña Mackenna's chapel/tomb, the noon cannon (*cañonazo*, historically) [BK]. At its foot, **Santa Lucía 162: ex-Clínica Santa Lucía, the DINA clinic and torture centre, now a National Monument** (plaque 2016) [DOC].
- **Metro:** Santa Lucía (L1 1977); Bellas Artes (L5 2000).
- **2005 / 2019 / 2025:** a tourist and courting spot in 2005. In 2019 the protests swirl around its foot. By 2025 it has security problems and restricted hours, plus Indigenous re-naming debates (Huelén/Welén) [BK — verify].
- **Game notes:** the only **solid rock** in the old centre (see file 03 §4.4).

### 3.4 Plaza Italia / Plaza Baquedano (and its 2019–2020 transformation)
- **Where:** the junction where the Alameda becomes Av. Providencia, Vicuña Mackenna runs south, and the Pío Nono bridge crosses north to Bellavista. The point where comunas Santiago, Providencia and (across the river) Recoleta meet [BK]. **Built where the Mapocho used to fork** [DOC].
- **Names:** officially **Plaza Baquedano** (equestrian statue of General Manuel Baquedano, 1928, over the **Tomb of the Unknown Soldier**) [BK]. Popularly **Plaza Italia**, after the Italian community's monument [BK]. Renamed **"Plaza de la Dignidad"** by protesters in 2019 [BK].
- **Landmarks:** the roundabout and statue. The **Torre Telefónica** (1996, the "cellphone tower", screen for Delight Lab's 2019 light projections) [BK]. **Parque Balmaceda** and the buried **Museo de los Tajamares** [REP]. The Centro Arte Alameda cinema (burned in Dec 2019) [BK]. GAM to the west. **Clínica Santa María** just across the river [DOC]. **Hospital del Trabajador** a block south-east [DOC].
- **Metro:** **Baquedano** (L1 1977; L5 1997).
- **2005:** the city's **party and protest square**: football wins, election nights (Bachelet's victory crowds, Jan 2006), student marches. Its roundabout is a traffic nightmare at rush hour. Costanera Norte has just opened under the river nearby (Apr 2005) [DOC].
- **~2019:** **the epicentre of the estallido.** From 18 Oct 2019 it hosts daily clashes and Friday mass protests. On **25 Oct 2019** the largest march in Chilean history fills it and the Alameda (≈ 1.2 M people) [BK]. The statue is painted, climbed, set on fire, and finally **removed for restoration on 12 Mar 2021** [BK]. Baquedano station is closed for long periods. Allegations circulate of a room in the station used to mistreat detainees; they are contested [BK]. **Eye injuries** from police pellets become a national scandal, and many are treated at the Hospital del Salvador's ocular trauma unit [BK]. Volunteer **"brigadas de salud"** (protest medics) set up first-aid points [BK]. Gustavo Gatica is blinded near here in Nov 2019 [BK].
- **2020–2025:** an empty plinth, fences and grey paint, recurring protest anniversaries. Government project **"Nueva Plaza Italia"**: **the roundabout eliminated, 8,750 m² of new green and pedestrian space, investment CLP 26,148 million**; works were expected to start in May 2024 [REP]. In 2025 the redesign is in progress and the statue's return is undecided [BK — verify state].
- **Game notes:** a mythic hinge between eras. The Founder's 2005 network grows around a party square. The Heir's 2025 inheritance sits beside a scarred, half-rebuilt monument. Under it lie the river's old fork, Line 1 and Line 5, and the Costanera Norte tunnel to the north (see file 03).

### 3.5 Barrio Bustamante and Parque Bustamante
- **Where:** the south-west corner of Providencia, running south from Plaza Italia between Vicuña Mackenna and Barrio Italia [BK — check on a map].
- **Character:** mid-century mid-rise apartments, students and young professionals, old residents. The long linear **Parque Bustamante** has a skate park, dog runs, and the municipal **Café Literario** library-café [BK]. Quiet streets one block from the madness.
- **Metro:** Baquedano (L1/L5), Parque Bustamante (L5 1997), Santa Isabel (L5 1997).
- **2005:** sleepy and middle-class; the Line 5 stations are 8 years old.
- **~2019:** tear gas drifts into the flats. Neighbourhood *cabildos* (open political assemblies) meet in the park. Graffiti everywhere [BK].
- **2025:** densified with new mid-rises; a popular rental zone; the L5 platforms are packed.
- **Game notes:** good **staff housing** for the Founder's nurses and paramedics; a walk to the Hospital del Trabajador.

### 3.6 The Hospital del Trabajador block (Ramón Carnicer)
- **Where:** **Ramón Carnicer 185, Providencia** [DOC], a block or two south-east of Plaza Italia. ACHS corporate offices are on the same street (No. 163) [BK]. Metro Baquedano is nearby.
- **Owner:** **ACHS**, the Asociación Chilena de Seguridad, a private non-profit *mutualidad* (employers' work-accident insurer under Ley 16.744 of 1968; founded 1958) [BK]. Registered as **high complexity**; accredited Mar 2024–Mar 2027 [DOC].
- **History:** construction began 1969 and it opened in **1971** [REP]. It was built for **work accidents and occupational disease** and later opened to the general public as **"ACHS Salud"** [REP]. A 2016 modernisation was "the largest architectural project in 45 years" [REP]. A **new ED on floor −1 with 35 boxes** opened in **2025**, and a **new pedestrian ER entrance in 2026** [DOC].
- **Capabilities:** trauma and orthopaedics, **burns unit** [REP], hand and microsurgery, spinal injury, rehabilitation [BK]. Heliport: unverified.
- **Character of the block:** ACHS ambulances from building sites, men in work boots and hard hats in the waiting room, union reps, insurance paperwork. At 07:00, shift change and a coffee cart.
- **2005:** the "workers' trauma hospital", little known to the public outside mutual coverage.
- **~2019:** sits in the estallido's zero zone. Access is complicated on protest days. It is a natural refuge for injured passers-by [BK — plausible, not verified].
- **2025:** a renewed basement ED (floor −1), more public patients; ACHS Salud branding.
- **Game notes:** the fiction places bunkers "around Hospital del Trabajador". Real anchors: a **deep, recently rebuilt basement ED**, the Metro L1/L5 tunnels under Plaza Italia, and the river-fork geology (see file 03, hook 4).

### 3.7 The Posta Central – UC – San Borja quarter
- **Where:** just south of the Alameda between Av. Portugal and Marcoleta, south-west of Plaza Italia [BK — check exact boundaries].
- **Landmarks:**
  - The **Posta Central (HUAP, Hospital de Urgencia Asistencia Pública)**, founded in **1911** as Chile's first emergency hospital [DOC]. Its present complex dates from ≈ 1967 [REP], plus the **Torre Valech** (simulation centre 2017) [DOC].
  - The **Hospital Clínico UC** (Marcoleta 367; begun 1937 as "Hospital del Corazón Misericordiosísimo de Jesús") [DOC].
  - The **Remodelación San Borja** towers (from 1969) and their **walkable service tunnels** (≈ 4–7.5 km, wells, boiler plant) [REP].
  - **Do not confuse** it with the *Terminal San Borja* bus station in Estación Central [BK].
- **Metro:** Universidad Católica (L1 1977), Santa Lucía (L1).
- **2005:** the Posta in its legendary-overcrowded era: stretcher queues, police guards, the national public reference for **adult major burns** (it became the formal national reference in 2007) [DOC]. The UC hospital is the elite academic Catholic hospital. San Borja's 1970s towers are ageing.
- **~2019:** the Posta receives estallido trauma; the San Borja residents live under tear gas.
- **2025:** the Torre Valech is running. San Borja tunnel tours come in 2026 [REP]. The hospital nexus of the centre.
- **Game notes:** **the densest real infrastructure for the fiction**: Chile's busiest adult trauma ED, an elite academic hospital, and a private tunnel utility all on adjacent blocks.

### 3.8 Providencia: Av. Providencia, Pedro de Valdivia, Los Leones, Tobalaba, "Sanhattan"
- **Where:** from Plaza Italia east ≈ 4 km to Tobalaba along Av. Providencia/11 de Septiembre (parallel one-way arteries), from the river south to Ñuñoa.
- **Character:** an orderly upper-middle-class commercial and office comuna. Galerías (shopping arcades) and old cinemas, embassies, hotels, apartment towers, leafy side streets of 1930s–50s houses (*casonas*) turned into clinics, consultancies and dental practices [BK]. **Sanhattan** (the business district straddling Providencia and Las Condes around Tobalaba, El Golf, Isidora Goyenechea and Nueva Las Condes) has glass towers [BK]. **Costanera Center** (mall 2012; Gran Torre Santiago ≈ 300 m; observation deck 2015) [BK].
- **Hospital cluster (south of Av. Providencia around Av. Salvador and José Manuel Infante):** the **Hospital del Salvador** (founded **7 Dec 1871**) [DOC], the **Instituto Nacional del Tórax**, the **Instituto de Neurocirugía Dr. Asenjo**, the **Hospital Luis Calvo Mackenna** (paediatric), and the **new Hospital del Salvador + Instituto Nacional de Geriatría** (641 beds, 98.5% built by May 2026) [REP]. Also the **Hospital Metropolitano** (ex-Hospital Militar), **Holanda 060**, next to Costanera Center [DOC].
- **Metro:** Salvador (L1 1977), Manuel Montt, Pedro de Valdivia, Los Leones, Tobalaba, El Golf (L1 1980); Los Leones also L6 (2017); Tobalaba also L4 (2005); Inés de Suárez (L6 2017).
- **2005:** **Labbé's Providencia**: clean, conservative, efficient, Christmas lights on Av. Providencia. Nightlife on **Suecia/General Holley** (fading) [BK]. The Hospital Militar is still on Holanda [DOC]. Costanera Norte has just opened [DOC]. Office boom around Tobalaba.
- **~2019:** the protests push east along Av. Providencia toward Los Leones and Tobalaba. Banks and pharmacies are boarded up; some looting. Costanera Center is closed on bad days [BK].
- **2025:** Matthei has left (presidential run); Bellolio is mayor [BK]. **Line 7 works** [REP]. The new **Hospital del Salvador** is finishing; the old pavilion hospital with its tunnels will empty. In May 2026 a patient escaped into those tunnels [REP]. The Hospital Metropolitano's MINSAL lease ends around 2026 [REP].
- **Game notes:** the **Founder's flagship** belongs here: the right postcode for Isapre patients, ambulance access to both the centre and the barrio alto, and Metro L1 beneath. A **casona on a side street off Pedro de Valdivia or Los Leones** is the classic private-clinic conversion.

### 3.9 Bellavista
- **Where:** north of the river between Pío Nono and the San Cristóbal hill. Split between **Recoleta (west of Pío Nono)** and **Providencia (east)** [BK].
- **Character:** colourful old houses, nightlife, the "schop" (draught-beer) bars of Pío Nono, restaurants, theatres. Neruda's house **La Chascona**; **Patio Bellavista** (2006) [BK]; the UChile Law School at Pío Nono 1 [BK]; the **funicular** up San Cristóbal [BK].
- **Clinics:** **Clínica Santa María** (Av. Santa María 0500 and the Bellavista 0415 tower with **5 basement floors and a heliport**, 2009) [DOC]; **Clínica Indisa** (Av. Santa María 1810) [DOC].
- **Metro:** none inside; Baquedano (L1/L5) across the bridge.
- **2005:** a party barrio; students; tourists going up to the zoo.
- **~2019:** adjacent to the zero zone. Nightlife collapses, then the pandemic finishes many bars [BK].
- **2025:** partial revival, with insecurity at night.
- **Game notes:** two major private clinics sit on the **riverbank right over the Costanera Norte tunnel corridor**.

### 3.10 Barrio Italia
- **Where:** around Av. Italia, on the Providencia/Ñuñoa border, south-east of Parque Bustamante [BK].
- **Character:** antique dealers and furniture restorers (2000s), then design shops, cafés, galleries and courtyard markets (2010s). Low houses with *patios*.
- **Metro:** Santa Isabel (L5 1997), Irarrázaval (L5 1997; L3 2019).
- **2005:** dusty restorers' workshops, cheap old houses. **The bargain zone for a Founder buying a casona.**
- **~2019 / 2025:** gentrified and pricier. Weekend crowds.

### 3.11 Ñuñoa
- **Where:** south of Providencia. Plaza Ñuñoa, Av. Irarrázaval, the Estadio Nacional.
- **Character:** middle-class, progressive-voting, families and students. Bars around **Plaza Ñuñoa**. The **Estadio Nacional** (1938; a mass detention site in 1973) [BK].
- **Metro:** **none in its core in 2005** (only L5 along its western edge) [BK]. L6 (2017): Ñuñoa, Estadio Nacional, Inés de Suárez. L3 (2019): Ñuñoa, Chile España, Villa Frei, Irarrázaval.
- **2005:** a bus comuna; quiet. **~2019:** the L3 opening in Jan 2019 transforms it; estallido assemblies in Plaza Ñuñoa. **2025:** densification and towers along the metro lines.

### 3.12 Barrio Brasil and Barrio Yungay
- **Where:** west of the Norte–Sur, from the Alameda north toward Mapocho; Plaza Brasil, Plaza Yungay, Matucana and the Quinta Normal [BK].
- **Character:** 19th-century elite mansions decayed into *pensiones* (boarding houses), *cités* (inner courtyards of tiny dwellings), universities and immigrant families. Barrio Yungay was declared a **Zona Típica** (heritage zone) in 2009 [BK].
- **Landmarks:** Plaza Yungay (the Roto Chileno monument); the ruined **Basílica del Salvador** (earthquake-damaged) [BK]; the **Biblioteca de Santiago** (opened 2005) and the **Museo de la Memoria y los Derechos Humanos** (2010) on Matucana [BK]; the Quinta Normal museums; the **Hospital San Juan de Dios** [BK]; the **ghost Metro station Libertad** [REP]; the **CESFAM Erasmo Escala** built over a historic sewer vault [DOC].
- **Metro:** República, Unión Latinoamericana (L1 1975), Los Héroes, Cumming and Quinta Normal (L5 2004).
- **2005:** students, artists, old people, Peruvian immigrants; cheap. **~2019:** protests; cité fires; the Venezuelan wave begins. **2025:** a large immigrant presence, overcrowded cités, frequent fires in old houses, pockets of gentrification.
- **Game notes:** **cheap old buildings with cellars**, close to the centre. Very high social-medicine load: TB, uninsured migrants, burns from cité fires, violence.

### 3.13 Estación Central
- **Where:** the comuna west of Santiago along the Alameda (created 1985) [BK].
- **Character:** **a transport hub**: the 19th-century railway terminus (the iron roof's attribution to Eiffel's workshops is popular legend, disputed) [BK], intercity bus terminals, the Mall Arauco Estación, USACH, the informal-commerce sprawl of Meiggs next door. The **"guetos verticales"** ("vertical ghettos"): dozens of 30–40-storey towers of tiny flats approved in the 2010s under a permissive plan, a national scandal [BK].
- **Health:** the **Mutual de Seguridad** hospital (Alameda ≈ 4848) and the **Clínica Bicentenario/RedSalud Santiago** next door [BK].
- **Metro:** Estación Central, Universidad de Santiago, Las Rejas, Ecuador and others (L1 1975). EFE commuter rail (Nos, 2017) [BK].
- **2005:** chaos, yellow buses, the smell of diesel and fried food. **~2019:** stations attacked, looting. **2025:** towers, crowds, crime, informal trade.
- **Game notes:** Améstica's informants say the old sewers run "from Plaza Italia toward San Alberto Hurtado" (file 03).

### 3.14 Independencia/Recoleta: the La Chimba hospital cluster
- **Where:** north of the river, along Av. Independencia, Av. La Paz and Av. Recoleta [BK].
- **Character:** the historic hospital and cemetery quarter. The Vega Central and the Patronato garment district (Korean, Palestinian and Peruvian traders) [BK].
- **Institutions:** **Hospital Clínico U. de Chile "J. J. Aguirre"** (Santos Dumont 999) [BK]; **Hospital San José**, **Hospital de Niños Roberto del Río**, the **Instituto Nacional del Cáncer** [BK]; the **Instituto Psiquiátrico Dr. Horwitz Barak** (ex-Casa de Orates) and the UChile **Facultad de Odontología**, **joined by a 1905–06 tunnel under Av. La Paz** [DOC]; the **Servicio Médico Legal** (the morgue) [BK]; **Clínica Dávila** (Av. Recoleta 464) [BK]; the **Cementerio General** [BK]; and on Av. General Borgoño, the **ex-Cuartel Borgoño**, CNI headquarters with basement cells, becoming a memorial site from 2025 [DOC].
- **Metro:** Puente Cal y Canto (L2 1987/L3 2019), Patronato, Cerro Blanco, Cementerios (L2), **Hospitales (L3 2019)**.
- **2005:** no metro to the hospitals (L3 arrives in 2019). Buses and walking. **~2019:** L3 opens, estallido protests. **2025:** gentrifying in parts, but a high crime perception remains.
- **Game notes:** a documented "**old underground network of hospital infrastructure**" (file 03 §4.9). The morgue and the CNI basements are a few blocks apart.

### 3.15 Las Condes, Vitacura, La Dehesa (brief)
- **The barrio alto.** Av. Apoquindo offices; Alonso de Córdova luxury boutiques; the Escuela Militar; Parque Arauco mall (1982) [BK]; the **Lo Curro** hills (the Pinochet mansion, now the Club Militar) [REP]; **La Dehesa** (Lo Barnechea) gated houses near the mountains [BK].
- **Health:** **Clínica Alemana** (Vitacura) [DOC]; **Clínica Las Condes** [BK]; **Clínica Universidad de los Andes** [BK]; **UC Christus San Carlos de Apoquindo** [REP]; **Hospital FACh**; **Hospital Dipreca**; **Clínica Tabancura** [BK].
- **Metro:** L1 to Escuela Militar (1980) and Los Dominicos (2010); L4 from Tobalaba (2005). **Vitacura has no Metro until L7 (~2028)** [REP].
- **2005:** Isapre clinics booming. **~2019:** the protests reach Apoquindo; residents frightened, gated. **2025:** San Martín mayor of Las Condes [BK]. The San Ramón fault runs under the eastern edge (file 03 §7.4).
- **Game notes:** where the **Old Men live** and where their families are treated. Ambulance runs come from here to the centre via Costanera Norte/Kennedy.

## 4. Hospital and clinic directory

### 4.1 How the system is wired (essentials) [BK]
- **Insurance.** **FONASA** (public insurer, roughly three-quarters of the population). **Isapres** (private insurers, ~15–18%; in crisis 2022–24 after court rulings, then the 2024 "Ley Corta"). The **armed forces and police** have their own systems (CAPREDENA, DIPRECA). **Mutualidades** (ACHS, Mutual de Seguridad CChC, IST) and the public **ISL** cover work accidents under **Ley 16.744**.
- **Public network.** Six **Servicios de Salud Metropolitanos**:
  - **Central**: HUAP, San Borja Arriarán, El Carmen (Maipú).
  - **Oriente**: Salvador, Tórax, Asenjo, Calvo Mackenna, Luis Tisné, Geriatría.
  - **Norte**: San José, Roberto del Río, INC, Horwitz.
  - **Occidente**: San Juan de Dios, Félix Bulnes.
  - **Sur**: Barros Luco, Exequiel González Cortés, El Pino.
  - **Sur Oriente**: Sótero del Río, Padre Hurtado, La Florida.
  - University hospitals (HCUCh, HC UC) sit partly outside this grid.
- **Emergencies.** Dial **131 SAMU**, 132 Bomberos (fire/rescue), 133 Carabineros, 134 PDI. The **Ley de Urgencia** (1999) obliges any hospital or clinic to stabilise a life-threatening emergency without demanding a guarantee [BK]. **AUGE/GES** (from Jul 2005) guarantees timely treatment for a growing list of conditions, which drives transfers between private and public [BK].
- **Private ambulances.** The 2005 market has private emergency-ambulance subscription services (e.g. Unidad Coronaria Móvil, Help) [BK]. The Founder is entering an existing, competitive niche.
- **2025 ownership shake-up.** UnitedHealth agreed in late 2025 to sell **Banmédica**: Clínica **Santa María**, **Dávila** and **Vespucio** plus the Banmédica and Vida Tres Isapres, ≈ US$1 bn. Reported buyer: **Patria Investments** (with Linzor). A Chilean filing names **Healthcare MX Holdings S.L.** [REP].

## 5. Travel times by car and ambulance

### 5.1 Method and caveats [EST]
- **No live routing data was available.** The times below are **estimates**, built from straight-line and road distances on the known street network and typical Santiago speeds:
  - night, ≈ 35–50 km/h on arteries and 60–80 on motorways;
  - midday, ≈ 20–25 km/h;
  - **rush hour** (07:00–09:30 and 17:30–20:30), ≈ 10–18 km/h on surface arteries and 25–40 on the motorways (which jam at their exits).
- **"Code 3"** (sirens and lights) helps on surface streets, perhaps 25–40% faster at peak, but not when every lane is jammed or a protest blocks the Alameda. Motorways (Costanera Norte, Kennedy, Vespucio) are the ambulance's friend at night and its trap at 18:30.
- Treat these as **dice-modifiable baselines**: rain, a Friday before a long weekend, a *Clásico* (big football derby), an Alameda march or a smog-restriction day all add time.

### 5.3 Other useful pairs [EST]
- **Posta Central ↔ Hospital Clínico UC:** a few hundred metres; a stretcher could be wheeled across.
- **Posta Central ↔ Hospital del Trabajador:** ≈ 2 km; 5 min at night, 15–20 at peak.
- **Providencia flagship (say Pedro de Valdivia) → Posta Central:** ≈ 4.5 km; 8–10 min at night, 25–35 at peak.
- **Providencia flagship → Clínica Alemana:** ≈ 6–7 km; 10 min at night, 20–35 at peak.
- **Barrio Brasil → San Juan de Dios:** ≈ 1–2 km.
- **La Pintana → Padre Hurtado:** ≈ 3–6 km, depending on the población (low-income housing district).

### 5.4 How traffic changed, 2005 → 2025
- **2004–2006: the motorway concessions** (Autopista Central, Costanera Norte, Vespucio Norte and Sur) with free-flow electronic **TAG** tolls and congestion-priced tariffs. They gave the city its first fast cross-town routes, especially **east–west under the river** [DOC/BK].
- **2007: Transantiago** (10 Feb 2007). A chaotic launch pushed many people into cars. Exclusive bus lanes and corridors followed [BK].
- **The car fleet in the Región Metropolitana roughly doubled** over the period [BK/EST]. Peak congestion spread from the classic corridors (Alameda–Providencia, Vicuña Mackenna, Kennedy, Vespucio) to the whole day.
- **Metro roughly doubled in length** (≈ 85 km by 2006 → ≈ 150 km by 2025) [BK]. This relieved some bus corridors but not the roads.
- **New tunnels:** Túnel San Cristóbal (2008), Kennedy and Lo Saldes (mid-2010s) [DOC]; AVO I (early 2020s) [BK].
- **Urban speed limit cut from 60 to 50 km/h** under the 2018 Ley de Convivencia Vial [BK].
- **2019–2020:** the estallido closed the Alameda and Plaza Italia for months, often unpredictably. Ambulances detoured via Santa María/Costanera or Matta/Bilbao. Then came **COVID quarantines and curfews (2020–21)**: empty streets and the fastest ambulance runs in modern memory [BK].
- **2025:** congestion at or above 2019 levels. **Line 7 works** disrupt Providencia and Vitacura [REP]. Seasonal **smog restrictions** (*restricción vehicular*) continue. The **25 Feb 2025 blackout** killed traffic lights citywide [BK].

## 6. Commercial real estate: costing the Founder's purchases

> **Everything in §6 is [EST].** These are reasoned order-of-magnitude figures from general knowledge of the Santiago market (brokers' annual reports from Colliers, CBRE, GPS and the like were not retrievable this session). Use them to keep the money plausible, not as quotations. The repository's `tools/dice.py money` converts UF/CLP/USD across years.

### 6.1 Units
- **UF (Unidad de Fomento):** an inflation-indexed accounting unit. All Chilean real estate is priced in UF, so **UF/m² comparisons across years are already in real terms**.
- **Conversions used here:**

| | 1 UF in CLP | 1 USD in CLP | 1 UF in USD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **2005** | ≈ 17,500 | ≈ 560 | ≈ 31 |
| **2019** | ≈ 28,000 | ≈ 700–800 | ≈ 35–40 |
| **2025** | ≈ 39,000 | ≈ 950 | ≈ 41 |

### 6.2 Price bands

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Land, 2005** (UF/m² of lot) | 6–14 | 10–18 | 12–25 | 20–35 |
| **Land, 2025** (UF/m² of lot) | 15–30 | 25–45 | 35–60 (higher on metro corners) | 50–90 |

**Market mood by year [BK/EST]:**
- **2005:** cheap, stable. The office market is migrating east; the centre is fading; Providencia side streets are full of convertible casonas.
- **~2013–2019:** a land and tower boom. **Peak land prices** in Providencia, Ñuñoa and Estación Central. Comunas respond by cutting heights in their planes reguladores (Providencia, Ñuñoa, Santiago).
- **2019–2021:** the estallido and COVID hit the centre and Plaza Italia edges hard. Vacancies spike and asking prices soften.
- **2025:** **office vacancy remains high post-pandemic** (often quoted around 10%+). Landlords offer incentives. The centre is the cheapest it has been in real terms for a decade. Providencia land holds value near Metro, and Line 7 station areas speculate upward.

### 6.3 Worked examples (for the Founder's spreadsheet)

**A. 2005: buy a casona for the first stabilisation unit (Plaza Italia edge, near Ramón Carnicer).**
- Lot 450 m², existing house 380 m² built, zoned for health use.
- Purchase ≈ 16–22 UF/m² of built area × 380 m² ≈ **6,000–8,400 UF** ≈ CLP 105–147 M ≈ **US$190–260k**.
- Conversion fit-out (resus bays, one emergency OR, 4 monitored beds, medical gases, generator, accessibility) ≈ 25–40 UF/m² × 380 ≈ **9,500–15,200 UF** ≈ US$300–470k.
- Equipment (monitors, 3–4 ventilators, an anaesthesia machine, portable X-ray, ultrasound, point-of-care lab, defibrillators, crash carts) ≈ **US$400–800k**.
- **All-in ≈ US$0.9–1.5 M** (≈ 29,000–48,000 UF).

**B. The same purchase in 2025.**
- Built ≈ 45–65 UF/m² × 380 ≈ **17,000–25,000 UF** ≈ CLP 670–970 M ≈ **US$0.7–1.0 M** for the property alone.

**C. Renting instead: 600 m² across ground and first floor on a Providencia side street.**
- **2005:** 0.30–0.38 UF/m² → 180–230 UF/month ≈ CLP 3.2–4.0 M ≈ **US$5.6–7.1k/month**.
- **2025:** 0.40–0.50 UF/m² → 240–300 UF/month ≈ CLP 9.4–11.7 M ≈ **US$9.9–12.3k/month**.

**D. A flagship building in Providencia, 2005.** A 4–5 storey 1970s office building of ≈ 2,000 m² near Pedro de Valdivia: ≈ 25–32 UF/m² ≈ **50,000–64,000 UF** ≈ **US$1.6–2.0 M**. The same building in 2025: ≈ 60–75 UF/m² ≈ 120,000–150,000 UF ≈ **US$4.9–6.2 M**. A Founder who bought in 2005 leaves the Heir a property worth roughly **2.5× in real terms**.

**E. A fresh lot for a purpose-built unit with deep basements, 2005.** A 600 m² lot in Providencia at ≈ 18 UF/m² ≈ 10,800 UF ≈ US$335k.
- New construction ≈ 30–40 UF/m² above ground (2005) and ≈ 15–25 UF/m² for each basement level (excavation, shoring, structure).
- Four basement levels of 550 m² ≈ 2,200 m² × 20 ≈ **44,000 UF ≈ US$1.4 M** before fit-out.
- In 2025 terms, multiply construction by ≈ 1.6–1.9 (real construction-cost inflation plus regulation) [EST].
- See file 03 §8 for how such basements can hide a bunker, and what the spoil trucks reveal.

### 6.4 Other cost benchmarks [EST, derived where marked]
- **A public hospital bed, fully built (2020s):** the new Hospital del Salvador cost ≈ US$400 M for 641 beds ≈ **US$620k per bed** [derived from REP figures].
- **Clinic construction (2025):** ≈ 55–80 UF/m² for the building alone; 1.5–2× that with full medical installations [EST].
- **Rule of thumb for the 2005 → 2025 escalation:**
  - land in central/eastern Santiago ≈ **2.5–3× in UF**;
  - built property ≈ 2.5×;
  - rents ≈ 1.3×;
  - construction ≈ 1.6–1.9×.
  - The CLP itself fell against the dollar (560 → 950), so **in USD the property multiples are ≈ 30% higher** than in UF terms.

### 6.5 Caveats for play
- Prices vary sharply within a block: corner lots on Metro exits, heritage restrictions (Inmuebles de Conservación Histórica cannot be demolished), and **zoning for health use** all matter. In Providencia a "clínica" use permit on a residential street is a political fight with neighbours.
- **Transaction costs:** notary and Conservador de Bienes Raíces (property registry) fees, broker ≈ 2%. Stamp tax applies to mortgages [BK].
- **Financing:** UF-denominated mortgages at ≈ 4–6% real in 2005, falling to ≈ 2–3% around 2019–20, then rising to ≈ 4–5% in 2023–25 [BK/EST].
