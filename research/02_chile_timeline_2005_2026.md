# 02 — Chile and Santiago, year by year: January 2005 → October 2026

> **How to read this file.**
> - It is a reference timeline for the DM, not fiction. Era I starts in 2005, Era II in 2025.
> - **≈** = approximate figure. **(?)** = the compiler is unsure of this detail.
> - **From mid-2025 every item carries a confidence tag:**
>   - **[High]**: confirmed by several outlets during compilation (Oct 2026).
>   - **[Med]**: one or two sources, or details still partly unclear.
>   - **[Low]**: the compiler's recollection or a single weak source. Verify before relying on it.
> - **Method note:** items with sources listed at the end were checked by web search in October 2026. Most pre-2025 items are standard, widely reported facts recorded from the compiler's knowledge and not re-checked one by one. The search budget ran out during compilation and page-fetching was unavailable, so some figures (especially exchange rates and some minimum-wage steps) are approximations, flagged as such.
> - **Money conventions:**
>   - **UF** (*Unidad de Fomento*): the inflation-indexed unit of account used for rents, mortgages, health-plan premiums, fines and contracts.
>   - **USD/CLP**: approximate annual average of the *dólar observado* (the Central Bank's daily reference rate), with notable spikes.
>   - **Minimum wage**: the *ingreso mínimo mensual* (gross, workers aged 18–65), with the date each step took effect.
>   - **Inflation**: the December-to-December CPI change.

## Standing context (true throughout Era I unless noted)

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Emergency numbers** | **131** ambulance (SAMU, the public pre-hospital service) · **132** Bomberos (all-volunteer fire brigades) · **133** Carabineros · **134** PDI (investigative police) |
| **Health system** | Two tiers. **FONASA** (public insurer) covers ≈70%+ of the population. **ISAPREs** (private insurers) cover ≈15–20% (≈), mostly in the barrio alto. Public hospitals and *consultorios* (primary-care clinics) are run by regional *Servicios de Salud* (the RM has six). Private clinics (Alemana, Las Condes, Santa María, Indisa, Dávila, etc.) serve ISAPRE patients and FONASA patients on the "*libre elección*" (free-choice) scheme. **Ley de Urgencia** (Law 19.650, 1999): a patient in *riesgo vital* (life-threatening danger) must be treated without prior payment; the State/FONASA pays the clinic and recovers costs. Demanding a cheque as a guarantee for emergency care was banned outright in 2009 (≈) |
| **Santiago transport** | Metro (Lines 1, 2 and 5 in early 2005; Line 4 from Nov 2005), *micros amarillas* (privately owned yellow buses) until Feb 2007, then **Transantiago** |
| **Political system** | 1980 Constitution as amended. **Binomial** electoral system (until 2015 reform, first used in 2017). Compulsory registration with compulsory voting until 2012, then automatic registration with voluntary voting until 2022, then **compulsory voting again** from the Sept 2022 plebiscite |
| **Presidents' terms** | 4 years from 2006 (no immediate re-election). Hand-over always on **11 March** |

## 2005: Lagos's last year
**Politics**
- **Ricardo Lagos** (PS-PPD, Concertación) ends his term with high approval (≈60%+).
- **Ley 20.050** constitutional reforms (Aug–Sept 2005). They abolish designated and lifetime senators, restore the president's power to remove the armed forces' commanders-in-chief, cut the presidential term to 4 years, and make the National Security Council advisory. Lagos signs the consolidated text on **17 Sept 2005**.
- **Elections, 11 Dec 2005:** Bachelet ≈46%, Piñera ≈25%, Lavín ≈23%, Hirsch ≈5%, so a run-off is needed (it is held in Jan 2006).
- In **March 2005** Colonia Dignidad's Paul Schäfer is arrested in Argentina. Arms caches are found at the colonia later in 2005.

**Disasters and mass casualties**
- **Antuco tragedy, 18 May 2005:** 45 conscripts (44 soldiers + 1 sergeant) freeze to death in a blizzard on the Antuco volcano during a forced march.
- **Tarapacá earthquake, 13 June 2005** (M≈7.8, deep): ≈11 dead in the north.

**Health**
- **AUGE/GES begins 1 July 2005** (Law 19.966). FONASA and ISAPREs must guarantee access, timeliness, financial protection and quality for an initial **25 conditions**. The list rises to 40 (2006), 56 (2007), 69 (2010), 80 (2013), 85 (2019) and 87 (2022) (≈).
- The Ley de Autoridad Sanitaria (2004) creates the Subsecretaría de Redes Asistenciales and the **Superintendencia de Salud** (2005).
- Winter smog episodes (*preemergencias*, pre-emergency alerts) bring vehicle restrictions in Santiago.

**Santiago**
- **Costanera Norte**, the urban motorway with a tunnel under the Mapocho riverbed, opens in stages. Free-flow **TAG** tolls start on the urban motorways.
- **Metro Line 4 opens 30 Nov 2005.**
- Transantiago's transitional phase begins (late 2005, ≈).

**Economy**
- UF 1 Jan ≈ **17,319** · USD ≈ **560** · inflation 3.7% · GDP ≈ +6%.
- Copper boom begins. Free-trade agreement with China signed.
- Minimum wage: **$120,000** → **$127,500** (1 July).

**Tech and culture**
- Prepaid mobile phones are near-universal. MSN Messenger and **Fotolog** (a teen photo-blog craze, huge in Chile 2005–07).
- *Machuca* (2004 film about the coup) is still discussed. Radio and TV news (TVN, Canal 13, Mega, Chilevisión) and *El Mercurio* and *La Tercera* set the agenda.

## 2006: Bachelet, the penguins and Pinochet's death
**Politics**
- **Run-off, 15 Jan 2006:** Michelle Bachelet ≈53.5% vs Sebastián Piñera ≈46.5%.
- **Inaugurated 11 Mar 2006**, the first woman president, with a gender-parity cabinet.
- **"Revolución pingüina"** (Apr–June 2006): secondary students in uniform (*pingüinos*) occupy schools, with a national strike at the end of May. This leads to the LGE education law (2009).
- First cabinet change, July 2006: Belisario Velasco to Interior.
- **Pinochet dies 10 Dec 2006** at the Hospital Militar in Santiago (≈ aged 91). Army honours at the Escuela Militar, no state funeral. Celebrations in Plaza Italia and mourning at the hospital.

**Disasters**
- **5 Mar 2006: the Edificio Diego Portales fire** destroys a third of the building's lower block. It is later rebuilt as the GAM cultural centre.

**Health**
- GES expands to 40 conditions (July).
- *Chile Crece Contigo* (early-childhood protection programme) launched (≈2006–07).

**Santiago**
- Metro Line 4A opens (Aug 2006).
- The Autopista Central, Vespucio Norte and Vespucio Sur motorways are operating.
- Costanera Center construction begins (≈).

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **17,974** · USD ≈ **530** · inflation 2.6%.
- Fiscal Responsibility Law (2006) creates sovereign funds (FEES).
- Minimum wage **$135,000** (1 July).

**Tech and culture**
- 3G mobile begins (≈ late 2006). Fotolog and MSN; YouTube arrives.
- **Gas cuts from Argentina** (2004–08) cause an energy anxiety.

## 2007: Transantiago
**Politics**
- **Transantiago chaos** drags Bachelet's approval down.
- **26 Mar 2007 cabinet change:** René Cortázar to Transport.

**Disasters**
- **Aysén fjord earthquake and landslide-tsunami** (21 Apr 2007): 10 dead (≈).
- **Tocopilla earthquake** (14 Nov 2007, M≈7.7): 2 dead, heavy damage in the north.

**Health**
- GES at 56 conditions (July).

**Santiago**
- **Transantiago launches all at once on 10 Feb 2007.** Too few buses, untested routes and an unready payment card (**bip!**). Hours-long waits, Metro Line 1 jammed at ≈6 passengers/m², and a political scandal lasting years.
- The yellow *micros* disappear overnight.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **18,335** · USD ≈ **522** · **inflation 7.8%** (food and energy).
- Minimum wage **$144,000** (1 July).

**Tech and culture**
- Facebook arrives and spreads. Reggaetón and "*pokemones*" (a teen emo/reggaetón subculture, 2007–08) dominate youth culture.

## 2008: volcano and global crisis
**Politics**
- Constitutional accusation removes Education Minister **Yasna Provoste** (Apr 2008).
- **Pension reform** (Law 20.255, Mar 2008) creates the *Pilar Solidario* (solidarity pillar), with the Pensión Básica Solidaria from 1 July 2008.
- **Municipal elections, 26 Oct 2008:** Pablo Zalaquett wins Santiago (≈).
- The Constitutional Court bans free public distribution of the morning-after pill (Apr 2008). It is later restored by law (2010).

**Disasters**
- **Chaitén volcano erupts, 2 May 2008.** The town is evacuated and buried in ash and lahars.

**Health**
- PBS pensions begin. Debate on emergency contraception.

**Santiago**
- Transantiago is slowly patched (more buses, a state subsidy).
- Smog alerts every winter.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **19,628** · USD ≈ **522** on average, but very volatile: ≈430 in April, ≈680 by Oct–Nov · inflation 7.1%.
- **Global financial crisis**: copper crashes from ≈US$4/lb to under US$1.50/lb (≈).
- Minimum wage **$145,000** (1 Jan) → **$159,000** (1 July).

**Tech and culture**
- **iPhone 3G arrives** (≈ Aug 2008). BlackBerry is the executives' phone.

## 2009: H1N1 and recession
**Politics**
- Bachelet ends with record approval (≈80%).
- **First round, 13 Dec 2009:** Piñera ≈44%, Eduardo Frei Ruiz-Tagle ≈30%, **Marco Enríquez-Ominami** (independent) ≈20%, Arrate ≈6%.

**Disasters and mass casualties**
- **H1N1 influenza pandemic.** First Chilean cases in May. Peak in June–July, when **winter emergency rooms and ICUs are saturated**. About 150 deaths (≈).

**Health**
- *Chile Crece Contigo* becomes law (Sept 2009).
- Ban on demanding cheques or guarantees for emergency care (≈ Nov 2009; Law 20.394 (?)).

**Santiago**
- Metro expansion works continue (Line 1 to Los Dominicos, Line 5 to Maipú).

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **21,453** (1 Jan). **Deflation** (CPI −1.4%) means the UF actually *falls* during 2009.
- USD ≈ **560** · GDP ≈ −1% · unemployment ≈10–11%.
- US$4 bn fiscal stimulus and one-off household bonuses.
- Minimum wage **$165,000** (1 July).

**Tech and culture**
- Twitter takes off among journalists and politicians.

## 2010: the earthquake, the miners and the prison fire
**Politics**
- **Run-off, 17 Jan 2010:** **Sebastián Piñera** ≈51.6% vs Frei ≈48.4%, the first elected right-wing president since 1958.
- **Inaugurated 11 Mar 2010** amid strong aftershocks (M≈6.9 that morning).
- **OECD membership** (accession signed Jan 2010, member May 2010).
- **Bicentenary**, 18 Sept 2010.
- Mapuche political prisoners' hunger strike (Jul–Oct 2010).

**Disasters and mass casualties**
- **27F: earthquake of 27 Feb 2010, 03:34**, **Mw 8.8**, epicentre off Cobquecura in the Maule region.
  - A **tsunami** devastates Constitución, Dichato, Talcahuano and Juan Fernández.
  - ≈**525 dead** and ≈25 missing (≈). Hundreds of thousands of homes damaged.
  - Failure of the tsunami-warning chain (SHOA/ONEMI), which leads to prosecutions.
  - Looting in Concepción. State of catastrophe with **curfews in Maule and Biobío**, troops deployed.
  - **In Santiago:** the airport terminal is damaged and closed for days, some modern buildings are evacuated or condemned, old adobe collapses, and power and phone networks are down for hours. Dozens of hospitals nationally are damaged, and several are evacuated.
- **San José mine collapse (Copiapó), 5 Aug 2010.** On 22 Aug the note "*Estamos bien en el refugio los 33*" ("We are fine in the shelter, the 33") reaches the surface. **All 33 miners are rescued 12–13 Oct 2010**, watched worldwide.
- **San Miguel prison fire, Santiago, 8 Dec 2010: 81 inmates die** in an overcrowded wing. Mass casualties of burns and smoke inhalation are taken to Santiago hospitals.

**Health**
- GES at 69 conditions.
- Law 20.418 (Jan 2010) guarantees access to contraception, including emergency contraception.

**Santiago**
- Metro Line 1 reaches Los Dominicos (Jan 2010).
- **GAM cultural centre opens** (Sept 2010).
- The Titanium La Portada tower is completed. The Costanera Center skyscraper is rising.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **20,939** · USD ≈ **510** · inflation 3.0% · GDP rebounds ≈ +6%.
- Minimum wage **$172,000**.

**Tech and culture**
- Smartphones and BlackBerry Messenger among the young.
- **Twitter becomes a disaster-information channel during 27F.**

## 2011: the student winter
**Politics**
- **Student movement** (Apr–Dec 2011): marches of 100,000+ on the Alameda and months of university and school occupations.
  - Leaders: Camila Vallejo, Giorgio Jackson, and later Gabriel Boric (FECh president, 2012).
  - 4 Aug 2011: a banned march, barricades and a city-wide *cacerolazo* (pot-banging protest).
  - **25 Aug 2011:** Manuel Gutiérrez, 16, is shot dead by a Carabinero in Macul during a national strike.
- HidroAysén dam protests (May).
- Piñera's approval falls to ≈26%. Cabinet changes (July).

**Disasters and mass casualties**
- **Cordón Caulle eruption** (June) brings ash and flight disruption.
- **Juan Fernández air crash, 2 Sept 2011:** an Air Force CASA 212 crashes, killing 21, including TV star **Felipe Camiroaga**. National mourning.

**Health**
- Postnatal parental leave extended to 6 months (Oct 2011).

**Santiago**
- Metro Line 5 reaches Maipú (Feb 2011).

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **21,456** · USD ≈ **484** (strong peso) · inflation 4.4% · GDP ≈ +6%.
- Copper peaks (≈US$4/lb).
- Minimum wage **$182,000**.

**Tech and culture**
- WhatsApp appears. Twitter becomes political.

## 2012: voluntary voting
**Politics**
- **Automatic registration with voluntary voting** (Law 20.568, Jan 2012).
- **Daniel Zamudio**, a young gay man, is beaten in **Parque San Borja** (3 Mar) and dies on 27 Mar. This leads to the **Ley Zamudio** anti-discrimination law (July 2012).
- Aysén regional uprising (Feb–Mar).
- **Municipal elections, 28 Oct 2012** (turnout ≈43%):
  - **Carolina Tohá** wins Santiago.
  - **Josefa Errázuriz** wins Providencia, unseating **Cristián Labbé**, ex-DINA and Pinochet's former bodyguard, mayor since 1996.

**Disasters**
- Torres del Paine fire (Dec 2011–Jan 2012).

**Health**
- **Patients' Rights and Duties Law** (Law 20.584, Oct 2012).
- Food-labelling law passed (in force 2016).

**Santiago**
- **Costanera Center mall opens** (June 2012).

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **22,296** · USD ≈ **486** · inflation 1.5%.
- Mobile number portability (Jan 2012).
- Minimum wage **$193,000**.

**Tech and culture**
- WhatsApp and Instagram spread. 4G spectrum auction.

## 2013: forty years after the coup
**Politics**
- **First legal primaries, 30 June 2013:** Bachelet ≈73% in the centre-left primary.
- The right's candidate, Longueira, withdraws (July). **Evelyn Matthei** replaces him.
- **40th anniversary of the coup** (Sept 2013): an outpouring of TV documentaries.
  - The judges' association apologises for failing victims.
  - Piñera speaks of "*cómplices pasivos*" (passive accomplices).
  - The luxury **Penal Cordillera** prison for military convicts is closed (Sept).
- **First round, 17 Nov 2013:** Bachelet ≈47%, Matthei ≈25%. **Run-off, 15 Dec 2013:** Bachelet ≈62%.

**Disasters**
- The central Chile **megadrought** (from 2010) deepens.

**Health**
- GES at 80 conditions (July).

**Santiago**
- The Gran Torre Santiago (Costanera Center) is topped out (≈), Latin America's tallest building at ≈300 m.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **22,837** · USD ≈ **495** · inflation 3.0%.
- Minimum wage **$210,000** (1 Aug).

**Tech and culture**
- 4G launches (≈2013–14).

## 2014: Bachelet II, Iquique and Valparaíso
**Politics**
- **Bachelet inaugurated 11 Mar 2014**, with the Nueva Mayoría coalition (now including the Communist Party).
- **Tax reform** (Sept 2014) and education reform.
- The **Penta** political-finance scandal breaks (late 2014).

**Disasters and mass casualties**
- **Iquique earthquake, 1 Apr 2014** (Mw≈8.2): 6 dead, coastal evacuations.
- **Great Fire of Valparaíso, 12–13 Apr 2014:** 15 dead and ≈2,900 homes destroyed on the hills.

**Security in Santiago**
- **8 Sept 2014: a bomb in the "Subcentro"** shopping gallery at **Escuela Militar** Metro station injures 14 (≈). Part of a wave of small anarchist bombings (2006–14).

**Health**
- The campaign for a high-cost-drugs fund grows after the death of journalist **Ricarte Soto** (2013).

**Santiago**
- The **Gran Torre Santiago** is complete. Uber arrives (≈2014).

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **23,313** · USD ≈ **570** · inflation 4.6% · GDP ≈ +1.8%.
- Minimum wage **$225,000** (1 July).

**Tech and culture**
- WhatsApp is universal. Uber and Netflix.

## 2015: Copa América and Caval
**Politics**
- **Caval case** (Feb 2015): Bachelet's son and daughter-in-law are implicated in a land deal, and her approval collapses.
- **Penta and SQM** illegal political-finance cases reach all parties.
- **Cabinet overhaul, 11 May 2015:** Jorge Burgos to Interior, Rodrigo Valdés to Finance.
- **Binomial system abolished** (Apr 2015).
- A constituent process is announced (Oct).

**Disasters and mass casualties**
- **Villarrica eruption** (3 Mar).
- **Atacama floods** (25 Mar): ≈30 dead (≈).
- **Calbuco eruption** (22 Apr).
- **Illapel earthquake, 16 Sept 2015** (Mw 8.3): tsunami, ≈15 dead.

**Health**
- **Ley Ricarte Soto** (Law 20.850, June 2015): a protection fund for high-cost diagnoses and treatments.

**Santiago**
- **Chile hosts and wins the Copa América.** In the **final on 4 July 2015 at the Estadio Nacional** Chile beats Argentina on penalties (Alexis Sánchez's chipped spot-kick). It is Chile's first major title, and Plaza Italia is packed all night.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **24,627** · USD ≈ **654** · inflation 4.4%.
- Minimum wage **$241,000** (1 July).

- **Water cuts** affect a large part of Santiago (Feb).

**Santiago**
- **Metro Line 6 opens** (2 Nov 2017).

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **26,349** · USD ≈ **649** · inflation 2.3%.
- Minimum wage ≈ **$264,000** (Jan) → ≈ **$270,000** (July) (?).

## 2018: Piñera II, the feminist May, Catrillanca
**Politics**
- **Pope Francis visits** (15–18 Jan 2018). The clerical-abuse cover-up scandal (Bishop Barros, the Karadima network) explodes, and in **May 2018 all Chilean bishops offer their resignation**.
- **Piñera inaugurated 11 Mar 2018.**
- **"Mayo feminista"**: university occupations against harassment.
- **Camilo Catrillanca**, a Mapuche man, is shot dead by Carabineros' special-forces "Comando Jungla" (14 Nov 2018). The cover-up is exposed.

- A migration wave arrives (Venezuelans, Haitians).

- **Quintero–Puchuncaví industrial gas poisonings** (Aug–Sept 2018): hundreds of schoolchildren and residents treated.

**Santiago**
- Metro Line 3 nears completion. Rising migrant communities in Santiago Centro, Estación Central and Independencia; the "vertical ghettos" (high-density towers) of Estación Central.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **26,799** · USD ≈ **641** · inflation 2.6% · GDP ≈ +4%.
- Minimum wage **$276,000** (1 Jan) → ≈ **$288,000** (mid-2018).

**Tech and culture**
- *Una mujer fantástica* wins the Oscar for foreign-language film (Mar 2018).

## 2019: the estallido social
**Politics and Santiago (the key year for the Plaza Italia setting)**
- **Metro Line 3 opens** (22 Jan 2019).
- On **6 Oct** the Metro fare rises by **$30**. Students organise **mass fare evasion** (from 14 Oct).
- **Friday 18 Oct 2019:**
  - The whole Metro shuts down. Commuters walk home through riots.
  - Metro stations are set on fire, and the **Enel headquarters** tower in the centre burns.
  - At midnight Piñera declares a **state of emergency**, and **soldiers patrol Santiago** for the first time since 1990.
- **19–27 Oct: curfews** in Santiago and other cities.
- **20 Oct:** Piñera: "*Estamos en guerra contra un enemigo poderoso*" ("We are at war against a powerful enemy").
- Supermarkets are looted. Neighbourhood **vigilante groups in yellow vests** guard streets, driven by WhatsApp panic rumours.
- **Metro damage:** most of the 136 stations damaged, ≈25 burned, ≈7 totally destroyed (≈).
- **25 Oct: "the largest march in Chile's history"**, ≈1.2 million people around **Plaza Italia**, renamed by protesters **Plaza de la Dignidad**.
- **28 Oct cabinet change:** Gonzalo Blumel replaces Andrés Chadwick at Interior. Chadwick was later constitutionally impeached and barred from office.
- **30 Oct:** the **APEC summit and COP25 are cancelled**.
- **8 Nov:** student **Gustavo Gatica** is blinded by police pellets at Plaza Italia.
- **12 Nov:** general strike and a very violent night.
- **15 Nov: Acuerdo por la Paz Social y la Nueva Constitución.**
- **26 Nov:** **Fabiola Campillai** is blinded by a tear-gas canister in San Bernardo.
- **Las Tesis**'s "*Un violador en tu camino*" goes global (late Nov).
- **Human cost (≈):** around 30 deaths associated with the unrest, thousands injured, and **400+ eye injuries** (INDH and Colegio Médico counts). UN, HRW and Amnesty reports (Dec 2019) document serious human-rights violations by Carabineros.
- Weekly Friday clashes at Plaza Italia continue until the pandemic. A "**primera línea**" ("front line") of shield-carrying youths holds the square. The area around the GAM, Parque Forestal, Lastarria, Av. Vicuña Mackenna and Av. Providencia's first blocks is tear-gassed, graffitied and shuttered for months.

**Health**
- The **Unidad de Trauma Ocular (UTO)**, the eye-trauma unit at the **Hospital del Salvador**, is overwhelmed with ocular trauma cases.
- Volunteer brigades run street first aid. Hospitals and clinics near Plaza Baquedano treat tear-gas exposure, pellet wounds and fractures.
- The Colegio Médico becomes a key critic of police munitions.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **27,566** · USD ≈ **703**. The peso falls to ≈830 in Nov, and the Central Bank intervenes with up to US$20 bn.
- Inflation 3.0% · GDP ≈ +0.8%.
- Minimum wage **$301,000** (1 Mar).

**Tech and culture**
- Live-streamed protests on Instagram and Twitter. Fake-news WhatsApp chains.

## 2020: COVID-19
**Politics**
- **3 Mar 2020:** first COVID-19 case in Chile.
- **18 Mar:** **state of catastrophe**, renewed until Sept 2021.
- **22 Mar:** **national curfew** (initially 22:00–05:00). It lasts in some form into late 2021 (≈).
- **26 Mar:** first lockdowns, which ironically hit the **barrio alto comunas first** (Las Condes, Vitacura, Lo Barnechea, Providencia, Ñuñoa, Santiago, Independencia), because infections arrived with travellers.
- **15 May:** **total quarantine for Greater Santiago** (lasting into July–Aug).
- **13 June:** Health Minister Jaime Mañalich resigns amid disputed death counts. **Enrique Paris** replaces him.
- The "**Paso a Paso**" ("step by step") plan sets comuna-by-comuna phases (July).
- **Pension withdrawals:** a first **10% AFP withdrawal** (July 2020), then a second (Dec 2020) and a third (Apr 2021).
- **Plebiscite, 25 Oct 2020:** **Apruebo** (approve a new constitution) **≈78%**, with a fully elected **Convención Constitucional** ≈79%. Turnout ≈51%.

**Disasters and mass casualties: COVID**
- **Santiago ICU occupancy reaches ≈95–98%** (June 2020).
- Critical beds roughly double nationally through conversion (≈).
- A centralised bed-management unit (UGCC) ships ventilated patients to other regions by **Air Force planes**.
- ≈16,000–23,000 deaths in 2020 (confirmed vs. including suspected) (≈).

**Santiago**
- Empty Alameda. Movement permits via the online "**Comisaría Virtual**" (police permit portal). A delivery-app boom (Rappi, PedidosYa, Uber Eats). Cacerolazos from balconies.

**Economy**
- UF ≈ **28,310** · USD ≈ **792**, spiking to ≈870 in March.
- GDP ≈ −6% · unemployment ≈13% at peak · inflation 3.0%.
- Emergency cash (IFE, the emergency family income).
- Minimum wage ≈ **$320,500** (1 Mar) → ≈ **$326,500** (1 Sept) (sources differ slightly).

**Tech and culture**
- Zoom, telemedicine, WhatsApp school groups.

## 2021: vaccines and Boric
**Politics**
- **Mass vaccination from 3 Feb 2021** (mostly Sinovac, then Pfizer and AstraZeneca). One of the world's fastest campaigns. The "**Pase de Movilidad**" (vaccine pass) arrives in May.
- **12 Mar 2021:** the **General Baquedano statue is removed** from Plaza Baquedano for restoration after repeated burning and vandalism. Only the plinth remains.
- **15–16 May mega-election** (Convention, governors, mayors): independents and the left surge. **Irací Hassler** (Communist Party) wins the Santiago mayoralty.
- **Convention installed 4 July 2021** (president: Elisa Loncón, Mapuche).

## Crime and security trend lines (Santiago, 2005–2025, qualitative)
- **2005–2015:** a low homicide rate (≈2.5–3.5 per 100k, among the lowest in Latin America). Crime fears focus on theft, *lanzazos* (snatch thefts), burglary and drug micro-trafficking in poblaciones such as La Legua (police intervention from 2001), La Victoria and Bajos de Mena.
- **2015–2019:** *portonazos* and *turbazos* (smash-and-grab mob robberies) emerge, and *barras bravas* (football hooligans) are a recurring issue.
- **2020–2024:** organised crime rises: Tren de Aragua, kidnappings for ransom, extortion of businesses, *narco-funerales* (gang funerals with gunfire). The homicide rate peaks at **≈6.7/100k (2022)** and falls slightly to ≈6/100k by 2024 (≈). Security becomes the top voter concern and the decisive issue of 2025.
