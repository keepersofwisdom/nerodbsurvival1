### 1.1 Sentence-level features (the music of Chilean speech)

- **Voseo verbal.** Chileans say *tú* but conjugate like old *vos*: *¿cómo estái?* (are you), *¿qué querí?* (do you want), *¿tenís?*, *¿podís?*, *¿sabís?*, *¿vai a ir?*, *¿dónde estái?*, *erís* or *soi* (you are), *¿cachái?*. Imperatives stay standard (*mira, ven, cállate*), with a negative *no seai weón*. The pronoun **vo** (*¿y vo qué?*) is **F**. Voseo is universal among peers (**I**); a doctor never uses it with a patient over ~40, and an older person who uses it with a stranger is picking a fight.
- **Usted** is used for elders, patients, strangers in service, and (in some families, old style) with one's own parents. Switching from *tú* to *usted* mid-argument is cold anger. Older nurses use *usted* with doctors; young teams use *tú*.
- **Po** (from *pues*) closes everything: *sí po, no po, ya po, obvio po, ¿y qué po?* Older rural speakers say *pues* or *pos*. Overusing *po* in a formal setting sounds uneducated; dropping it entirely sounds foreign.
- **Ya** means OK, yes, enough: *ya* (agreed), *¡ya po!* (come on!), *¿ya?* (got it?), *ya, ya, ya* (stop talking, I understand).
- **¿Cachái?** (from English *catch*): "you get it?" It's a tic, used at the end of every other sentence, by every class (**I**). Variants: *¿me cachái?*, *cacho* (I get it), *no cacho* (no idea), *cachar* (to understand / to catch someone out).
- **Weón / huevón (wn).** The master word. Literally "big-balled", it means: *dude* (affectionate, **I**); *the guy* (*el weón de la esquina*); *idiot* (*¡weón!*); intensifier (*¡qué weón más bacán!*). Derivatives: **weá** (thing/stuff: *pásame esa weá*; problem: *¿qué weá?*), **weonada** (stupid act), **weonear / huevear** (to mess around, to bother), **hueveo** (fun, fooling, hassle), **aweonao** (stupid), **la weá buena** (great), **puta la weá** (dammit), **a la weá** (to hell / whatever), **ni una weá** (nothing at all), **weona** (female). Tone carries the meaning: *wena, weón* (hi, mate) vs *weón culiao* (fucking idiot, **V**). Upper-class men say *güeón* with drawn-out vowels; teenage girls say *weona* to each other affectionately. On WhatsApp: *wn*, *weon*, *weá* → *wea*.
- **Diminutives** soften everything, especially requests and bad news: *un cafecito, un momentito, al tiro nomás, esperate un poquitito, una pastillita, un pinchacito* (a little prick, as nurses say before a needle), *le vamos a poner un suerito*. Over-diminutives are working-class and older-female tender; *cuicos* use them too, ironically.
- **Al tiro / altiro**: right now (really: soon, maybe). *Vuelvo altiro* = back in a minute (or an hour).
- **Nomás**: just, go ahead: *pase nomás* (come in), *sírvase nomás*.
- **Harto**: a lot, very: *harta gente, harto bueno*. Universal and very Chilean.
- **Súper / demasiado / terrible** as intensifiers: *súper rico* (all ages since the 90s), *demasiado bueno* ('97), *terrible bueno* (2010s youth), *la media weá* (huge thing, '97).
- **Aspiration and elision.** Final and pre-consonant *-s* aspirated or lost (*lo' niño'*); intervocalic *-d-* drops (*cansao, pescao, too*, i.e. *todo*). The ending *-ado* becomes *-ao* everywhere; in **F** speech, also *-ido* > *-ío*. **F** and rural speech pronounce *ch* as *sh* (*Shile, shoro*); it is mocked by *cuicos*. Speed: Chileans speak fast, swallow syllables, and are hard for other Latin Americans to follow.
- **Rising tone** at the end of statements (*cantadito*, sing-song), stronger in women and in the south.

### 1.3 Swearing (*garabatos*, *chuchadas*)
Chile is one of the most profane Spanish-speaking countries in casual speech. Swearing marks intimacy, not only anger.

### 1.4 How people sound: class and age voice sheets

**Upper class (*cuico*, Las Condes / Vitacura / Lo Barnechea / Chicureo)**
- Drawn vowels, a nasal lilt, *o sea*, *como que*, *súper*, *demasiado*, *full*, English sprinkled in (*el weekend, súper random, full feliz*); *¡qué rico!* about everything.
- Mentions: *el colegio* (by name), *el campo* (family estate), *Zapallar / Cachagua* (beach), *el sur* (lakes), *la Parva / Valle Nevado* (ski), *la nana* (live-in maid, often Peruvian), *el club*, *misa*, *Schoenstatt / Legionarios / Opus Dei*.
- Older cuico (born '45): *regio, estupendo, fíjate que, mi hijita*.
- *"O sea, no, como que el doctor de la clínica me dijo que era súper normal, ¿cachái?, pero igual fui donde el Pancho, que es primo de la Coté, que es como el jefe de urgencia de la Alemana."*

**Middle class (Ñuñoa, Providencia, La Florida, Maipú, Macul)**
- Default Chilean: *po*, voseo, *¿cachái?*, *weón* among friends, diminutives, euphemisms around bosses.
- *"Ya po, si salgo a las ocho del turno, nos tomamos una chela en Plaza Ñuñoa, ¿te tinca?"*

**Popular class (*poblaciones*: La Pintana, Puente Alto, La Granja, La Legua, La Victoria, Lo Espejo, Cerro Navia, Renca)**
- Stronger elision, *sh* for *ch*, more diminutives and *mijo/mija*, more evangelical or Catholic-folk formulas (*si Dios quiere, Diosito lindo*), *harto* everything.
- *"Doctorcito, mi guagüita no ha comío na' desde ayer, y le subió la fiebre, la tiene too calentito el cuerpo."*

**Flaite / street young men**
- *¿Qué mirai, loco? / ¿Y vo qué? / Ya hermano, piola. / Me bajaron el celu*. Lots of *culiao*, *loco*, *hermano*; trap slang (*pikete, flow, la lleva, las Jordan*).
- *"Hermano, ¿qué weá? Me tienen esposao a la camilla y ni me han pescao, loco."*

**Old people ('45) in the ED**
- Formal, polite, apologetic, euphemistic about bodies. *"Fíjese, doctor, que me vino una pálida en la feria, y la vecina me trajo. No quería molestar."* They call female doctors *señorita* and male nurses *doctor*.

**Evangelical (*canuto*)**
- *Hermano, hermana, gloria a Dios, aleluya, Dios le bendiga, bendiciones*; no swearing; Bible, suit and tie on Sunday; street preaching with megaphones in Plaza de Armas (*¡Cristo viene!*).

**Venezuelan, Haitian, Peruvian and Colombian Santiago (2010s–2020s)**
- Venezuelans: *chamo, pana, chévere, vaina, arrecho, ¿qué más?*. Chilean youth now borrow some of these. Haitians: Kreyòl among themselves, careful formal Spanish. Peruvians: *causa, pata, chamba, jato*, *chela* (which Chile already absorbed). Colombians: *parcero, ¿qué más pues?, berraco*.

### 1.5 How doctors talk

**To patients (always *usted* with adults, often *tú* with teens and children)**
- *"A ver, señora Rosa, cuénteme qué le pasó."* *"¿Desde cuándo?"* *"¿Le duele aquí? ¿Y aquí?"* *"Respire hondo... bote el aire... tosa."*
- *"Le vamos a tomar unos exámenes y le vamos a poner un suerito con algo para el dolor."* (patients **expect** *suero*, an IV drip, and an *inyección para el dolor*, and feel cheated without one)
- *"Va a quedar en observación." / "Va a tener que hospitalizarse." / "Lo vamos a derivar al especialista." / "Pida hora en su consultorio para control."*
- Bad news: *"Hicimos todo lo posible..."*; *"Su papá falleció"* (never *murió* to the family). Older doctors still soften with *"el corazón se cansó"*.
- Lay medical talk to recognise: *me dio una pálida* (faint), *la presión* (hypertension: *soy de presión alta*), *el azúcar* (diabetes: *me subió el azúcar*), *un preinfarto*, *un derrame* (stroke), *la puntada* (stabbing pain), *colerina* (diarrhoea), *la tiritona* (shivers), *fatiga* (old: faintness or nausea), *empacho* (folk indigestion), *mal de ojo*, *la mollera caída* (folk paediatric), *estar con la regla / me vino* (period), *lumbago*, *me duelen los riñones* (low back pain), *quedé con la boca chueca*.
- Popular demand: *la licencia* (sick note). The *licencia médica* is a weapon, a currency and a source of fraud (*licencias truchas* scandals).

**To each other (fast, acronym-dense, black humour, *tú* and *weón*)**
- *"Paciente de 74, HTA, DM2 insulinorrequiriente, ERC etapa 4, consulta por disnea de tres días, llega saturando 84, MEG, LOTEP, crépitos bibasales. Le pedí gases, BNP, Rx de tórax. Lo dejé con BIPAP."*
- *"Llegó chocado"* (in shock), *"está séptico"*, *"lo intubamos"*, *"le pasé un litro de suero fisiológico"*, *"está con DVA"* (vasoactive drugs), *"quedó para pabellón"*, *"sube a la UCI"*, *"está para UTI"*, *"lo dejamos en la intermedio"*, *"se fue"* / *"se nos fue"* / *"hizo exitus"* (died), *"llegó en paro"*, *"recuperó pulso a la tercera"*, *"el curadito del box 6"*.
- To a nurse: *"Dame un ketorolaco y una clorfenamina EV, porfa."* To an intern: *"Interno, evolucione la cama 4 y pida la interconsulta a cirugía."*

## 2. Chilean hospital and medical slang and culture

### 2.1 The system in one paragraph
Two systems live side by side. **FONASA** (public insurer, ~75–80% of the population, approx.; income bands A–D) pays for the public network: **CESFAM / consultorio** (primary care), **SAPU / SAR** (primary-level urgent care, evenings and weekends; SARs open 24 h), and the big public hospitals run by Santiago's six **Servicios de Salud** (Norte, Occidente, Central, Oriente, Sur, Sur-Oriente). **ISAPREs** (private insurers: Banmédica, Colmena, Consalud, Cruz Blanca, Vida Tres, Nueva Masvida) cover the richer ~15–20% (approx.) and pay for **clínicas** (private hospitals: Clínica Alemana, Clínica Las Condes, Santa María, Indisa, Dávila, UC Christus, Bupa, Meds, RedSalud). Milestones: **Ley de Urgencia** (1999, Ley 19.650: no cheque or guarantee may be demanded before a vital emergency is stabilised; later reinforced in 2009, approx.); **Plan AUGE/GES** (Ley 19.966, in force July 2005: guaranteed coverage for a growing list of conditions); **Ley de Derechos y Deberes del Paciente** (Ley 20.584, 2012); **Ley Ricarte Soto** (2015, high-cost treatments); **EUNACOM** (Ley 20.261, 2008); **Ley Karin** (Ley 21.643, in force Aug 2024: workplace harassment; named after Karin Salgado, a TENS who died by suicide after harassment at a Chillán hospital). The **Isapre crisis** (Supreme Court rulings 2022, *ley corta* 2024) is a constant 2020s background noise.

Emergency numbers: **131 SAMU** (ambulance), **132 Bomberos** (fire brigades, **all volunteers**: a Chilean peculiarity), **133 Carabineros**, **134 PDI**. Private ambulance-subscription services (*rescate*, e.g. HELP, Unidad Coronaria Móvil; approx. names) are well known among the middle class, which matters for the protagonist's business.

### 2.6 The culture of the 24-hour shift
- **Shape of a public ED 24 h (approx.):** 08:00 handover, coffee and gossip; mid-morning the *consultorio* referrals and old people who "didn't want to bother anyone" arrive; afternoon traffic accidents; evening *colación* (often eaten standing); 22:00–03:00 the drunk, the stabbed, the overdoses, the domestic violence, Carabineros with detainees; 03:00–06:00 the dead hours, broken by an arrest or a pulmonary oedema; 06:00 the *residencia* empties, notes are finished, the *saliente* looks grey.
- **Contracts:** public ED doctors classically work under **Ley 15.076** shift contracts (the "28-hour" *cargo de turno*, i.e. one 24-h shift a week plus hours, approx.) or **Ley 19.664** daytime contracts; many top up with *horas extra* and private shifts. Private clínicas pay **por hora** or **por turno** on a *boleta de honorarios* (freelance invoice, with tax retained at ~10% rising to ~15% through the 2020s, approx.). A typical Chilean doctor works **public in the morning, clínica in the afternoon, and sells shifts at weekends**.
- **Seasonal peaks:** **Campaña de invierno** (June–August: RSV, flu, pneumonia; corridors full; ICU beds scarce); **Fiestas Patrias** (18–19 Sept: drunk drivers, knife fights, burns from *asados*, kids cut by glass-coated kite string (*hilo curado*), choking on meat); **New Year** (stray-bullet *balas locas* from celebratory gunfire, fireworks burns); **summer** (drownings in rivers and pools, heat strokes, bike crashes).
- **Wild nights:** football *clásicos* (Colo-Colo vs U de Chile), protest days (2006, 2011, and above all the **Estallido of Oct 2019–Mar 2020**, when the Hospital del Salvador's eye-trauma unit treated hundreds of pellet eye injuries), the 27F earthquake (27 Feb 2010, 03:34), the **COVID** winters (2020–21: field hospital at Espacio Riesco, patients flown to other regions in FACh aircraft, ICU beds doubled, *residencias sanitarias*).
- **Violence against staff:** assaults by relatives are common (punched TENS, smashed boxes); security guards and police posts in EDs; stronger penalties under a 2019 law (approx.).
- **Dark humour (texture, not a script):** *"la noche de los cuchillos largos"* (18 Sept), *"el Tapsin con ketorolaco cura todo"*, *"la abuelita de las 4 a.m. que 'no quería molestar' y tiene un infarto"*, *"el paciente conocido del servicio"* (frequent flyer), *"el policonsultante"*, *"se fue de alta voluntaria por la ventana"*, *"en el pasillo no muere nadie: se muere en la camilla 12-B"*; nicknames for regulars (*el Rey del OH, la señora de las convulsiones*). Class-coded jokes about *flaites* and *cuicos* are frequent and ugly; a good DM lets them be heard and lets someone wince.
- **Doctors and nurses:** nurses (*EUs*) run the floor and remember everything; a young doctor who disrespects the *EU jefa* or the old TENS loses the shift. The lore says *"la TENS sabe más que el interno"* (often true). Romances between doctors and nurses are a standing joke; so is the surgeon's ego. Interns carry samples, fetch films, write notes and are sent to buy the *completos*. **Pagar el piso**: a newcomer brings cake or pizza. Birthdays get a *torta* in the *estar*.
- **Public vs private culture:**

| Public hospital | Private clínica |
|---|---|
| "*Aquí se ve de todo*": huge volume, everything pathological, the best training | Fewer and milder cases, more demanding patients, "*el paciente es cliente*" |
| Old buildings, waiting lists, shortages, *paros* (strikes) by FENPRUSS/FENATS, corridors | Marble, coffee bar, VIP rooms, lawyers |
| Patients grateful or furious; *"doctorcito"* | Patients who call their cousin, the medical director |
| Pride: *"yo me formé en la Posta"* | Pay 1.5–3× higher per hour (approx.) |
| Hierarchical, political, union culture | Corporate, productivity metrics, *metas* |

### 2.7 Med school and career
- **Seven years** (14 semesters); the last two are the **internado** (rotations in internal medicine, surgery, paediatrics, OB-GYN, psychiatry, ED, rural or primary care). Graduates hold the title **Médico Cirujano** (Physician-Surgeon). Entry by national exam: **PAA** (to 2002) → **PSU** (2003–2020) → **PTU** (2021–22) → **PAES** (2023–). Medicine takes the top scorers.
- **U. de Chile ("la Chile")**: public, oldest, Faculty in Independencia (campuses attached to public hospitals: Norte/J.J. Aguirre, Oriente/Salvador, Sur/Barros Luco, Centro/San Borja Arriarán, Occidente/San Juan de Dios). Secular, left-leaning, *tomas* (occupations) and strikes, mixed classes, fierce public-hospital pride.
- **PUC ("la Católica", "la Cato")**: Marcoleta and the Hospital Clínico UC; Catholic, elite, research-heavy, polished, partly conservative; strong private-sector links.
- **USACH**: public, more middle-class, solid. **U. de los Andes** (Opus Dei, upper-class conservative). **UDD** (Clínica Alemana, upper class). **U. Mayor, U. Andrés Bello (UNAB), U. San Sebastián, Finis Terrae, U. Diego Portales**: the private boom of the 2000s; once derided (*"compraron el título"*). The **EUNACOM** ranking became the great equaliser or exposer.
- **EUNACOM** (*Examen Único Nacional de Conocimientos de Medicina*; Ley 20.261, 2008; first sat 2009, approx.): theory plus practical sections; required to work in the public system and to apply for specialty training, **and** for foreign-trained doctors to practise. The score ranks you for residency (*becas*). Foreign doctors (Venezuelan, Cuban-trained, Colombian, Ecuadorian, Bolivian) often drive Ubers, work as TENS or sell in pharmacies while studying for it.
- **After graduation:** (1) **EDF / médico general de zona** (posted to rural hospitals for 3–6 years, then a funded *beca*); (2) **CONISS** and the national *concursos* for MINSAL-funded residencies (with a **PAO**, an obligation to return service years); (3) self-funded residencies; (4) straight into ED shifts as a *médico general*.
- **Specialty prestige (folk ranking):** money and lifestyle in **dermatology, ophthalmology, radiology, anaesthesia, plastic surgery, traumatology (*traumato*), urology**; prestige in **neurosurgery, cardiac surgery, cardiology, ICU, oncology**; respected but poorer in **internal medicine, paediatrics, psychiatry, family medicine, geriatrics**. **Emergency medicine** rose from "something GPs do" to a real specialty during 2005–2025.
- **Student culture:** *mechoneo* (hazing), *carretes de fin de semestre*, *pasar ramos*, *el ramo filtro* (weeding-out course: anatomy), student debt (**CAE**, the state-guaranteed loan, 2006–), **gratuidad** (free tuition for lower-income students from 2016), the 2006 *Revolución Pingüina* and 2011 student protests.

### 2.8 Drugs on every Chilean ED shelf (brand names doctors and patients use)
*Ketorolaco* (the default ED analgesic), *metamizol* (patients say **baralgina** or *dipirona*), *diclofenaco* IM (*la inyección en la nalga*), *tramadol* (*tramal*), *clorfenamina* (antihistamine), *metoclopramida* (*Pramidin*), *ondansetrón*, *omeprazol*, *salbutamol* (*el puff*), *Tapsin* (OTC cold remedy), *Kitadol* (paracetamol), *Viadil* (antispasmodic), *Ravotril* (clonazepam: *la ravotril*; misused, *la clona*), *zopiclona*, *losartán* and *enalapril* (public-system antihypertensives), *metformina*, *insulina NPH*. Pharmacy chains: **Cruz Verde, Salcobrand, Ahumada** (the 2008–09 price-collusion scandal); **Dr. Simi** (Mexican, dancing mascot); **farmacias populares** (municipal cheap pharmacies from 2015, Recoleta).

## 3. Money and prices by year

### 3.1 Units of account
- **Peso (CLP, $)**. Coins: $10, $50, $100, $500 ($1 and $5 vanished from use; cash totals are rounded to the nearest $10 since ~2017, approx.). Notes: $1,000, $2,000, $5,000, $10,000, $20,000. Polymer *billetes de plástico* came in 2009–2011 (approx.).
- **UF (Unidad de Fomento)**: inflation-indexed unit, updated daily, used for rents, mortgages, Isapre premiums, fines, big contracts. *"El arriendo es de 15 UF."* Newspapers print *el valor de la UF hoy*.
- **UTM**: monthly tax unit, used for traffic fines.
- **Sueldo líquido vs bruto**: net vs gross. Employees lose ~10% plus commission to their **AFP** (pension fund) and 7% to health (FONASA or more to an Isapre). Freelancers issue a **boleta de honorarios**.
- **Slang:** *luca* = 1,000; *gamba* = 100 (old); *quina* = 500 (old); *palo* = 1,000,000; *medio palo* = 500,000.

### 5.2 TV and big events
- **Festival de Viña del Mar** (every **February**, Quinta Vergara amphitheatre): the *Monstruo* (the crowd that boos and devours comedians), the *Gaviota de Plata/Oro* awards, the **Queen of the Festival** thrown into the hotel pool (*el piscinazo*), the red-carpet *Gala*. Everybody watches; next morning the ED waiting room discusses the comedian who bombed.
- **Teletón** (late Nov or early Dec, 27 hours, Don Francisco, since 1978): national ritual of charity and *chovinismo*; corporate donations, the *meta* countdown, mocked by the young and wept over by the old.
- **Programmes by era (approx.):** 2005: *Morandé con Compañía*, *Rojo*, *Mekano*, *Tolerancia Cero*, *Los Simpson* daily on Canal 13, *Casado con hijos*; gossip *Primer Plano*. 2008–12: *Yingo* and *Calle 7* (teen reality shows), *Los 80* (Canal 13: a middle-class family under the dictatorship), *Mundos Opuestos* (2012), *El Señor de la Querencia*; *Teletrece* and *24 Horas* for news. 2014–19: *Pituca sin lucas*, *Perdona nuestros pecados*, *Verdades ocultas* (Mega soap empire), morning shows *Bienvenidos*, *Mucho Gusto*, *Buenos días a todos*; *En su propia trampa* (sting show). 2020s: *Podemos hablar*, *Pasapalabra*, *Gran Hermano Chile* (2023–24), *Tierra Brava* (2023).
- **National events (for the "where were you" texture):** Bachelet first woman president (Mar 2006); *Revolución Pingüina* (2006); Transantiago chaos (Feb 2007); **27F earthquake and tsunami** (27 Feb 2010, Mw 8.8); Bicentenario (Sept 2010); **33 miners** rescued (13 Oct 2010); 2011 student protests; Copa América titles (2015, 2016); Pope's visit and church sex-abuse scandal (Jan–May 2018); **Estallido social** (18 Oct 2019); COVID (first case Mar 2020); constitutional plebiscite (Oct 2020, 78% *Apruebo*); first draft constitution rejected (Sept 2022, 62% *Rechazo*); second rejected (Dec 2023); Viña del Mar mega-fire (Feb 2024, 130+ dead, approx.); the Santiago windstorm blackout (1–2 Aug 2024, some homes dark for over a week); the **national blackout of 25 Feb 2025** (from ~15:16, Arica to Los Lagos, ~98% of the population: Metro stopped, traffic lights dead, hospitals on generators, a night curfew); presidential election 2025 (José Antonio Kast won the December runoff against Jeannette Jara; took office Mar 2026).

### 5.3 Football
- **Colo-Colo** ("el Cacique", *los albos*, white): the people's club, champions of the 1991 Libertadores; *la Garra Blanca* (ultras); stadium Monumental (Macul). Won four straight titles 2006–07 under Claudio Borghi.
- **Universidad de Chile** ("la U", *los azules*, blue): *Los de Abajo* (ultras); champions of the **Copa Sudamericana 2011** under Sampaoli (Eduardo Vargas). The **Superclásico** Colo-Colo vs U is a riot risk.
- **Universidad Católica** ("la UC", *los cruzados*): the "*cuico*" club; stadium San Carlos de Apoquindo (Las Condes); four straight titles 2018–21.
- **La Roja (national team):** World Cups 2010 (Bielsa) and 2014 (Sampaoli; Pinilla's shot off the crossbar against Brazil, a national trauma); **Copa América 2015** (champions at home, final 4 July 2015 at the Estadio Nacional vs Argentina, Alexis Sánchez's chipped decisive penalty); **Copa América Centenario 2016** (champions again vs Argentina, New Jersey, 26 June 2016). The *Generación Dorada* (Bravo, Medel, Vidal, Alexis, Aránguiz, Isla, Beausejour, Vargas). Then decline: failed to qualify for 2018, 2022 and 2026. Before 2015 Chile had **never** won a senior title, and older men cried in the streets. On match nights EDs fill with drunk fans, car crashes on Plaza Italia, and heart attacks.
- **Pichanga** on Saturdays, *baby fútbol*, *la Liga* among doctors and firemen; *el asado después*.

### 5.6 Dating culture
- 2005: meeting at university, work, *carretes*, MSN and Fotolog comments (*firmar el flog*), chat rooms; *pololeo* formalised with a declaration (*"¿quieres pololear conmigo?"*), meeting the parents at Sunday lunch.
- 2010s: Facebook stalking, WhatsApp double ticks (*me dejó en visto*), **Tinder** from ~2014, Happn, Bumble; *andar* vs *pololear*; *amigos con ventaja*; living together (*convivir*) before or instead of marriage; divorce only legal since **2004** (Ley 19.947); civil union (*Acuerdo de Unión Civil*) 2015; same-sex marriage from **March 2022** (Ley 21.400).
- **Motels** (*moteles*, rooms by the hour) are normal for couples who live with their parents (most young Chileans live at home into their late twenties).
- Hospitals: doctor–nurse and doctor–doctor *pololeos* are common and gossiped about in the *estar*; the *saliente* date is a running joke (the doctor falls asleep at dinner).

### 6.2 Home food
**Pastel de choclo** (corn crust over *pino*, chicken, egg: summer); **humitas**; **cazuela** (clear soup with a piece of meat, whole potato, piece of squash, corn on the cob: the cure-all); **porotos granados** (fresh beans, corn, basil: summer), **porotos con riendas** (beans with spaghetti: winter, poor); **charquicán**; **lentejas**; **carbonada**; **pollo arvejado**; **pastel de papas**; **caldillo de congrio** (Neruda's ode); **paila marina**; **machas a la parmesana**; **ceviche** (Peruvian influence); **asado** (weekend barbecue: *longaniza* from Chillán, *costillar*, *prietas* (blood sausage), *choripán*); **pollo asado con papas fritas** (Friday delivery); **pebre** (tomato, onion, coriander, chilli: on everything); **merkén** (smoked Mapuche chilli).

**Bread:** Chile eats among the most bread per capita in the world (approx. 80–90 kg/yr). **Marraqueta** (*pan batido*, *pan francés* in the south): crusty four-lobed roll, bought warm at 7 p.m.; **hallulla**: flat round roll; **pan amasado**: home-style, from a *carrito* or the *amasandería*; **dobladitas**.

**Once** (pronounced *ON-seh*): the 6–8 p.m. tea-meal of bread, *palta*, ham, cheese, butter, *manjar*, jam, tea (Chileans drink a lot of tea: *un tecito*), maybe *kuchen* or *queque*. For many working families *once* replaces dinner. *Tomar once* is a social institution (*"Venga a tomar once"*). Maite Alberdi's documentary *La once* (2014) is about it.

**Sweets:** *manjar* (dulce de leche) in everything, **kuchen** (German south), *torta de mil hojas*, *torta tres leches*, *brazo de reina*, *alfajores*, *chilenitos*, *leche asada*, *sémola con leche*, *pie de limón*, *Super 8* (wafer bar), *Sahne-Nuss*, *Frac*, *Tritón* biscuits, *Ramitas*, *Kapo* juice packs, *Bilz y Pap* (fizzy drinks), *Cachantun* (mineral water).

### 6.4 Hospital food and night-shift eating
- **Colación**: the staff meal, a tray in **el casino** (canteen): *cazuela*, *porotos*, *pollo con arroz*, *jalea* (jelly) for dessert. Night staff may get a *colación fría* (sandwich, juice box, fruit).
- **El régimen**: patients' diets (*régimen liviano, hiposódico, diabético, cero*). The patient's *régimen liviano* tray (pale chicken, mashed potato, *jalea*) is the shared joke.
- **Night shift:** *completos* from the *carrito* outside the ED gate; *sopaipillas* from the vendor on the corner at 22:00; Nescafé by the jar; *pan con palta* and *manjar* in the *estar de enfermería*; a box of *chilenitos* from a grateful family; instant noodles (*Maruchan*); vending-machine *Super 8*; from the 2010s, delivery by app at 02:00 (pizza, sushi, *pollo frito*, *shawarma*) handed through the ED door by a Venezuelan rider; *Red Bull* and Monster in the 2020s. Doctors at private clínicas get a decent cafeteria and complain anyway.
- *Pagar el piso* (newcomer brings cake), birthdays with a *torta* from the bakery, Fiestas Patrias *empanadas* on shift, Christmas *pan de Pascua* and a *cola de mono* hidden in a saline bottle (the classic prank).

## 7. City life

### 7.1 Weather and air (Mediterranean, dry, continental-tinged, ringed by mountains)
| Season | Months | Feel |
|---|---|---|
| **Summer** | Dec–Feb (and March) | Dry heat, 29–33 °C afternoons, 12–15 °C nights; heatwaves to ~37–38 °C in record years (approx.: 2017, 2019, 2023–25); burning sun, little shade in the poor south-west; empty city in February (*el éxodo* to the coast); Andes almost bare of snow; forest-fire smoke some years |
| **Autumn** | Mar–May | Golden plane trees (*plátanos orientales*), first rains in May, cool mornings, *alerta ambiental* season starts (April); March = back to school and work, traffic returns |
| **Winter** | Jun–Aug | Cold mornings 0–5 °C, **frost** in the outer comunas, afternoons 12–17 °C; rain fronts (*sistema frontal*) bring flooding in low streets and *canales* overflowing; **after rain, the Andes appear blindingly white over the city** (the most beautiful day of the year); **smog** (*la nube de smog*) on still days: **alerta / preemergencia / emergencia ambiental**, with **restricción vehicular** by last digits of the licence plate (*dígitos*), extended to cars with catalytic converters (*sello verde*) on bad days, and wood-stove bans; houses are badly insulated (gas or paraffin heaters, *estufas a parafina* smelling of kerosene, *guateros*, blankets) |
| **Spring** | Sep–Nov | Warming, windy; **plane-tree pollen** (Sept–Nov) torments allergy sufferers; purple jacarandas in November; Fiestas Patrias kites in September |

- **Rain**: historically ~300 mm/yr, nearly all May–Aug; a **megadrought** since ~2010 left some years far below (2019 among the driest on record, approx. <100 mm). A wet winter is news.
- **Snow in the city** is rare and joyous: notable falls in 2007, 2011 and the big one of **15 July 2017** (approx. dates for the first two), which broke thousands of trees and cut power.
- **Earthquakes** (*temblores*): Chileans don't call it a *terremoto* unless it's catastrophic; they guess magnitudes (*fue un 6, por lo menos*), stand in doorways, and laugh at foreigners who run. Big ones: 3 March 1985 (the '45 and '70 generations remember), **27 Feb 2010 (27F)**, 16 Sept 2015 (Illapel). Every building has a *zona de seguridad* sign. **ONEMI** became **SENAPRED** (2023). This is a prepper-friendly culture: *mochila de emergencia*, water, radio, torch.
- **Puelche**: dry wind from the Andes; *raco*: east wind in winter that clears the smog.

### 7.2 Sounds and smells
- **Sounds:** the gas trucks (**Lipigas, Abastible, Gasco**) clanging spanners on cylinders as they cruise; the knife-grinder's (*afilador*) pan-pipe whistle; the *organillero* (barrel organ) with a *chinchinero* (drum-and-cymbal dancer) in the centre; the noon cannon on **Cerro Santa Lucía** (*el cañonazo de las doce*); *"¡Compro, compro, compro! ¡Fierro, cachureo!"* from junk carts; *micro* vendors (*"Señor pasajero, disculpe la molestia..."*, then candy at "*tres por mil*"); evangelical megaphones in Plaza de Armas; street dogs barking at motorbikes; car alarms; *cacerolazos* (pot-banging protests: 1980s, 2011, 2019); helicopters over Plaza Italia; *bocinazos* after a win; narco fireworks at midnight in some *poblaciones* (a shipment has arrived, or a gangster is being buried); cicadas (*chicharras*) in summer; the lapwing (*queltehue*) screaming in parks (folk lore: it announces rain); Metro chimes and *"Próxima estación..."*.
- **Smells:** winter wood smoke and diesel; fried *sopaipillas*; warm bread at dusk; the Mapocho in summer (river and sewage); cut grass and sprinklers in Vitacura; paraffin heaters; the Metro's warm rubber; jasmine and lime-blossom in spring; *asado* smoke on Sundays; tear gas lingering for days in 2019–20 around Plaza Italia.
- **ED smells:** alcohol gel and chlorine; urine and vomit; the iron of blood; *hálito alcohólico* (beer and *piscola*); melaena (doctors know it from the door); infected diabetic foot (*pie diabético*); burnt hair; the sour sweat of the homeless; wet wool in winter; the clean cold of a private clínica.

### 7.3 Transport
- **Pre-2007: the yellow micros** (*micros amarillas*, 1992–2007): privately owned, drivers paid per passenger, racing each other (*la guerra del centavo*), loud cumbia, hanging passengers, schoolchildren's half fare; a thousand routes painted on the windscreen; the driver taking coins and giving paper tickets.
- **Transantiago** (launched **10 Feb 2007**): planned integration with the Metro, card-only payment (**Bip!**), trunk and feeder buses. Launch was a catastrophe: missing buses, hours-long waits, Metro crushes, a national scandal. Slowly fixed; renamed **Red** (*Red Metropolitana de Movilidad*) from 2019. Massive fare evasion (*evasión*: jumping through the back door) is a 2010s–2020s norm.
- **Metro**: clean, efficient, packed. In 2005: Lines 1, 2, 5; **Line 4** opened Nov 2005 (4A in 2006); Line 1 to Los Dominicos (2010); Line 5 to Maipú (2010–11); **Line 6** (Nov 2017); **Line 3** (Jan 2019); Line 7 under construction (approx. 2027). **Peak crush** on Line 1 eastbound 7–9 a.m.: pushed in by staff, faces against glass. On **18 Oct 2019** stations were set on fire; the network was crippled for months.
- **Colectivos**: shared taxis on fixed routes, ~1.5× a bus fare, beloved of night-shift workers in outer comunas.
- **Taxis**: black with yellow roofs; from 2014 at war with **Uber**, Cabify, DiDi, Beat (2017–21 approx.), InDrive.
- **Bicycles**: *ciclovías* from the 2010s; *bicis públicas*; delivery riders.
- **Highways**: urban toll motorways (*autopistas urbanas*: Costanera Norte, Vespucio, Autopista Central, Kennedy) since ~2004–06 with **TAG** electronic toll tags. Rush hour on Costanera Norte; *el taco de la Kennedy*.

### 7.4 Fiestas Patrias, Christmas and the holiday calendar
- **Fiestas Patrias (18–19 Sept, *el Dieciocho*)**: *fondas* and *ramadas* (Parque O'Higgins, every comuna), *cueca*, *chicha*, *terremotos*, *empanadas*, *anticuchos*, kites (*volantines*; banned glass-coated *hilo curado* still cuts children and motorcyclists), *rayuela*, *trompo*; the **Te Deum** (18th, cathedral) and the **Parada Militar** (19th, televised); a 3–5 day national bender; ED carnage.
- **Christmas (*Pascua*)** in summer heat: **Viejito Pascuero** (Father Christmas) in a wool suit at 32 °C, *pan de Pascua*, *cola de mono*, presents opened at midnight, the stampede in **Barrio Meiggs** for cheap toys; plastic trees, cotton snow.
- **New Year**: Valparaíso fireworks (the big one), **Torre Entel** fireworks in Santiago; rituals: lentils in the pocket, yellow underwear, eating twelve grapes, walking round the block with a suitcase to travel in the new year.
- **Public holidays (*feriados*)**: 1 Jan; Good Friday and Holy Saturday; 1 May (Labour Day); 21 May (Glorias Navales); ~20–21 June (**Día Nacional de los Pueblos Indígenas**, from 2021); 29 June (San Pedro y San Pablo, moved to a Monday); 16 July (Virgen del Carmen, from 2007); 15 Aug (Asunción); 18 and 19 Sept (sometimes the 17th or 20th added); 12 Oct (Encuentro de Dos Mundos, moved); 31 Oct (Iglesias Evangélicas y Protestantes, from 2008); 1 Nov (Todos los Santos); 8 Dec (Inmaculada Concepción); 25 Dec. Election days are holidays. Retail **feriados irrenunciables** (1 May, 18–19 Sept, 25 Dec, 1 Jan). *Fin de semana largo* = the motorway exodus and a Monday of crash victims. *Interferiado* = bridge day.
- **Other rituals:** pilgrimage to **Lo Vásquez** (7–8 Dec; thousands walk Route 68 overnight to the sanctuary: SAMU and Red Cross posts all along); **Cuenta Pública** (the president's state-of-the-nation, 21 May until 2017, 1 June since); **Día del Joven Combatiente** (29 March: night of barricades in some *poblaciones*); **11 de septiembre** (coup anniversary: unrest, vigils, burning barricades, pellet injuries).

### 7.5 Class markers
- **Comunas:** *barrio alto* / *sector oriente* (Las Condes, Vitacura, Lo Barnechea, La Reina, upper Providencia; new money in **Chicureo** gated suburbs); middle (Ñuñoa, Providencia, Macul, La Florida, Maipú, San Miguel, Santiago Centro flats); popular (Puente Alto incl. **Bajos de Mena**, La Pintana, La Granja, San Ramón, El Bosque, Lo Espejo, Pedro Aguirre Cerda incl. **La Victoria**, San Joaquín incl. **La Legua**, Cerro Navia, Renca, Pudahuel, Quilicura). "*De Plaza Italia para arriba*" means rich. The *cota mil* (homes at ~1,000 m on the foothills) is the top.
- **Schools:** cuico private (Grange, Saint George's, Verbo Divino, Tabancura, Craighouse, Colegio Alemán, Villa María, Santiago College, Los Andes); *liceos emblemáticos* (Instituto Nacional, Liceo 1, INBA, Liceo de Aplicación, Carmela Carvajal) with their protests and *tomas*; *subvencionados* (state-subsidised private) for the middle; municipal for the poor. **"¿En qué colegio estudiaste?"** is the classic class-sorting question.
- **Surnames** (see §8): a double compound upper-class surname is a passport; a Mapuche surname still costs job interviews.
- **Labels:** *cuico*, *flaite*, *picante*, *rasca*, *siútico*, *arribista*, *facho pobre*, *roto*, *ABC1*. *Nana* (live-in domestic worker; often Peruvian from the 2000s) and the "*puertas adentro*" arrangement; separate entrances and bathrooms in some homes.
- **Visible markers:** the *cuico*'s North Face jacket, loafers, sweater over shoulders, Volvo, the *pelolais* hair; the *flaite*'s Nike Air Max or Jordan, tracksuit, flat-brim cap, chain, motorbike; the middle class in *buzo* on Saturday, Falabella clothes, Hyundai, house in La Florida, *vacaciones en el sur*.

### 7.6 Religion
- **Catholic** (declining sharply: from ~70% in the 2002 census to roughly half by the 2020s, approx.); devotions to **Virgen del Carmen** (patron of Chile), **Santa Teresa de los Andes**, **San Alberto Hurtado** (Hogar de Cristo); *mandas* (vows to saints); *animitas* (roadside shrines where someone died violently, often with candles, plaques and football scarves; folk saints like Romualdito near Estación Central). The **Karadima** abuse scandal (2010–11) and the Pope's 2018 visit led to the whole bishops' conference offering its resignation, which broke the Church's authority.
- **Evangélicos/Pentecostals** (~15–18%, approx., heavily popular-class): storefront churches (*la iglesia*, *el culto*, *el pastor*, *el diezmo*), street preaching, Sunday suits, no drink or swearing. They run rehab homes for addicts. Own holiday 31 Oct.
- **No religion**: rising fast among the young and the educated (approx. a quarter to a third by the 2020s).
- Others: Jewish (Ñuñoa, Las Condes), Muslim (small; the Palestinian community is mostly Christian Orthodox), Mapuche spirituality (*machi*, *We Tripantu* at the winter solstice).

### 7.7 Crime and police
- **Carabineros** (green uniforms; *pacos*, *los verdes*): national uniformed police, militarised, prestigious until the 2017–19 scandals (*Pacogate* embezzlement, *Operación Huracán* fabricated evidence, the 2018 killing of Camilo Catrillanca, the 2019 eye-injury repression). Vehicles: *radiopatrulla*, *furgón*, **zorrillo** (tear-gas truck, "skunk"), **guanaco** (water cannon); special forces (*FF.EE.*), **GOPE** (SWAT).
- **PDI** (*Policía de Investigaciones*: plain-clothes detectives; *ratis*, *tiras*). **Fiscalía** (prosecutors): the adversarial criminal-procedure reform reached Santiago in June 2005; before that, written inquisitorial trials. **Gendarmería** runs prisons; detainee-patients are guarded by *gendarmes*.
- **Crime vocabulary:** *portonazo* (carjacking at your own gate as you open it), *encerrona* (boxed in at a light and robbed), *turbazo* (a mob swarms a shop or house), *motochorro* (thieves on motorbikes snatching phones), *lanzazo* (snatch), *cogoteo* (chokehold mugging), *alunizaje* (ramming a car through a shop window), *robo por sorpresa* (legal term for snatching), *bala loca* (stray bullet), *sicariato* (contract killing), *secuestro* (kidnapping, rising 2022–), *extorsión*, *gota a gota* (Colombian-style loan-sharking), *narcofuneral*.
- **Arc of fear:** 2005: petty crime, some *poblaciones* controlled by local drug clans (La Legua under police "intervention" from 2001); 2010s: *portonazos*, *turbazos*, *motochorros*, a middle-class panic about crime; 2020s: transnational gangs (**Tren de Aragua** and others), shootings, kidnappings, homicide rates rising to historic highs (still below most of Latin America), three Carabineros killed in Cañete (Apr 2024), and security dominating politics. The 2025 election turned largely on crime and migration.

### 7.9 Nightlife and sex culture
Nightlife begins late (dinner 21:30, *previa* at home, bar at midnight, club 01:30–05:00, *completo de bajón* at dawn). Sex culture: Catholic surface, practical underneath; motels for privacy; *cafés con piernas*; massage parlours (*casas de masajes*) in downtown buildings; sex work legal but unregulated, street work in parts of Santiago Centro; the **morning-after pill** controversy (2006–10); abortion banned outright until **Ley 21.030 (2017)** allowed it in three cases (risk to life, fatal foetal anomaly, rape); before that, clandestine abortions with misoprostol (*Misotrol*) came to EDs as "*aborto en evolución*". LGBT: Bellavista scene, Marcha del Orgullo; murder of **Daniel Zamudio** (2012) led to the anti-discrimination *Ley Zamudio*; gender-identity law (2018); same-sex marriage (2022). HIV rates rose in the 2010s among young men.
