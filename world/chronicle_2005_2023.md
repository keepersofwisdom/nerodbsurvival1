# CHRONICLE OF THE FOUNDER — Santiago, 2005–2023

*Era I, as it happened, written at the player's direction in November 2026 (real time) when
play moved to Era II. Real history is as it was; the Network, its people and its bunkers are the
fiction. Everything in this file is what **Rodrigo Bastías** knew or lived, so the player knows
it. **Pietro does not**: in play, he learns it only by finding it. Truths Rodrigo never learned
are kept by the DM.*

---

## Part I — La Primera Piedra (2005–2009)

### 2005: the money, the building, the arch
- **June.** Rodrigo raised his 4,800 UF at the last minute. He sold the Providencia flat at a
  discount (≈2,000 UF) and half his MedSur stake to Pato (≈2,000 UF, "en cuotas", of which Pato
  never paid the last two). The other ≈800 UF were his parents' savings. Hernán asked who else
  was lending. When Rodrigo said *Larrañaga*, his father went very still and said nothing more
  that night. On **Thursday 9 June 2005**, at the notary on Huérfanos, Hora Dorada S.A. bought
  the **Edificio Caja Austral**. Carolina signed the mortgage authorisation in her architect's
  capitals.
- **Fase 1** was chosen: one theatre and four ICU beds to start, the rest later. Pincheira was
  kept on as night watchman; it seemed cruel not to, and Rodrigo had come to like the old
  sergeant's thermos and radio.
- **July–August.** Works began: the "seismic retrofit", two new basement levels and the
  generator room, with spoil trucks lost among the Bicentennial plaza's until December. Carolina
  signed the permit drawings for −3 as "bodegas y recintos técnicos". She'd seen the real ones.
- **Late September.** Digging −3 at the north-east corner, the excavator broke into concrete
  that wasn't on the 1938 plans: the hatched "estanque". Behind it was a stair, and behind the
  stair a room. That night Rodrigo, Carolina and Pincheira went down with torches and Salinas's
  dead lamp, out of superstition. **B2, "El Archivo"**:
  - a 1980s shelter for about forty, damp in one corner;
  - bunks, tinned rations dated 1983, a dead ventilation unit with Swiss labels;
  - filing cabinets: **La Mesa's** accounts from 1979 to 1990, minutes of meetings, and a list
    of twelve names, typed, with crosses beside eight of them;
  - plans for two other shelters, marked **B1** and **B2**. The folders for **B3** and **B4**
    were there, labelled and **empty**;
  - a ledger in a numerical code, 1985–87, which nobody could read.
  
  Rodrigo photographed three of its pages with Gonzalo's little Canon.
- At the east end of B2 a gallery ran towards the Plaza de la Constitución, **walled up in 1990**.
  Through a ventilation slot in the wall, by torchlight, there were **footprints in the dust on
  the far side**.
- **November.** Benavente came with two men and took the ledger away, *"para ordenarlo"*.
  Larrañaga called it *"un malentendido de ingenieros"* that the doctor had found the old room
  "antes de tiempo". Salinas, ashamed, said only: *"Le dije que no todo iba a ser nuevo."* In
  December the plaza beside La Moneda opened, and the spoil cover was gone.

### 2006: Clínica Toesca opens
- **5 March.** The Edificio Diego Portales burned two kilometres east; Toesca, not yet open, sent
  its first ambulance to stand by.
- **3 April 2006: Clínica Toesca opened** (Fase 1), with an ED of three resus bays and twelve
  boxes, one theatre, four ICU beds, two physician-staffed advanced ambulances, and the courtyard
  bay Carolina had seen on the first Sunday. The first patient was a sixty-year-old court clerk
  from Tribunales with a heart attack, who walked in because the clinic was on his way to work.
- **Seba** became medical director. **Ximena Pizarro** became head nurse, and **Toño** from SAMU
  became ambulance chief.
- **The first year of the model.** Physician regulation of every call, as SAMU did. Rapid-sequence
  intubation in the ambulance, which the anaesthetists hated. "Stabilize and transfer" as a written
  protocol with the receiving clinics. Godoy at the Posta called it *"una ambulancia con
  pretensiones"*. Rodrigo kept one shift a week at the Posta until 2007, then let it go.
- Rodrigo and Benavente **resealed the 1990 wall** in concrete, from La Mesa's side. Nobody asked
  who had made the footprints, out loud.
- **10 December.** Pinochet died at the Hospital Militar. Larrañaga went to the rites at the
  Escuela Militar and came back looking twenty years older.

### 2007: Transantiago, and the motorcycles
- **10 February.** Transantiago launched and the city seized. Toesca's ambulances took ninety
  minutes to cross the centre. Rodrigo's answer was paramedics on **motorcycles** ("las motos"),
  two of them by March and eight by the end of the year. A first responder reached the patient
  in minutes; the ambulance arrived when it could.
- **October.** Salinas sold Hora Dorada the **old wing of the Clínica Tajamar** on Ramón
  Carnicer, by Plaza Italia, for a symbolic price, *"porque tiene que ser de un médico"*. Beneath
  it lay **B1, "La Capilla"**, the cleanest of La Mesa's shelters: dry and sealed, room for sixty,
  and a second access through a Metro Line 5 emergency shaft that had been "decommissioned" in
  1997.
- **December 2007.** Salinas, eighty-four and on anticoagulants, fell in his house. Señora Inés
  called Rodrigo before she called the ambulance. A subdural. He died in Toesca's ICU three days
  later. His last lucid word, said to Rodrigo alone, was ***"Yungay"***. Rodrigo wrote it in a
  notebook and never found out what it meant.

### 2008: the Plaza Italia unit, and the storage unit in Quilicura
- **Unidad Plaza Italia** opened in the old Tajamar wing, a block from the Hospital del
  Trabajador. **B1 was restored** beneath it over two years with Benavente's drawings: filtration,
  water, a generator, bunks.
- **Toesca reached full build**: two theatres, eight ICU beds.
- **The Quilicura case.** Thieves broke into the storage unit Rodrigo rented in Quilicura and were
  caught selling morphine ampoules in Barrio Meiggs. The PDI traced the unit to Dr. Rodrigo
  Bastías. For six weeks he waited to be destroyed. Then Covarrubias, Prieto & Asociados, whom
  he hadn't called, took the case, and it ended in a *suspensión condicional* with no record and
  no press. Larrañaga never mentioned it, which was how Rodrigo knew it was a debt. The cellar's
  controlled drugs went into Toesca's pharmacy, logged. Gonzalo and Carolina learned the whole
  story then.

### 2009: H1N1, and the ventilator
- **The H1N1 winter** filled Toesca's ICU. On **14 July 2009** a "new" ventilator from MedSur
  failed in the night on a thirty-four-year-old woman with H1N1 pneumonia, and she died. The
  machine was refurbished and sold as new, in breach of the pact's warranty clause.
- The family sued. *La Tercera* ran it for a week.
- **Pato** was bought out in 2010 at book value minus damages. MedSur went bankrupt in 2011 and
  Pato left for Miami. Rodrigo and Gonzalo split his shares. The two old friends haven't spoken
  since.
- The **UGCC**, the state's bed-management unit, began buying Toesca's ICU beds that winter, and
  never stopped.

## Part II — La Hora Dorada (2010–2016)

### 2010: 27F
- **January.** **Unidad Estación Central** (2009) and **Unidad La Florida** (Jan 2010) were open.
  La Florida had a small shelter of Rodrigo's own beneath its generator room, for twenty.
- **27 February 2010, 03:34. The earthquake.** In Santiago, Toesca's generator started in eight
  seconds; Rodrigo had tested it every Sunday for four years. The Plaza Italia unit's did too.
  - **The first night:** the Network ran the triage of central Santiago. Old adobe in Yungay
    and Brasil had collapsed, the airport was closed, and phones were dead. The Network's radios
    and motorcycles were the only things that worked.
  - **On the 28th:** Rodrigo took a convoy south with SAMU: four ambulances and a truck he'd
    fitted as a **mobile stabilization unit**, to Talca and Constitución. They worked eleven days
    among the tsunami's dead.
  - **What it made him:** Rodrigo came back with a second scar, and he was famous. The Minister
    invited him to the commission on disaster health response. **Standing (Medicine, State and
    Press) all rose.**
- **The clause.** On the night of the 27th, Larrañaga, eighty-nine, arrived at Toesca's carriage
  gate at five in the morning with his daughter, two grandchildren and a nurse, and asked for
  "nuestro recinto". The state of catastrophe covered Maule and Biobío, not Santiago, so the lease
  didn't oblige Rodrigo to open. He opened anyway. They slept two nights in −3 during the
  aftershocks. Larrañaga thanked him in Latin and sent the staff a hundred *empanadas*.
- **After 27F, Seba won the argument about "la lista"**: the Network's shelters (B1 under Plaza
  Italia, La Florida, and the space Rodrigo had in B2) would take **the staff and their families
  first**. Seba and Ximena typed it. At its longest, in 2012, it had **318 names**.
- **8 December.** The **San Miguel prison fire**: eighty-one dead. Network ambulances carried the
  burned to the Posta all night.

### 2011–2013: the school
- **March 2011. Larrañaga died**, aged ninety-one. At the funeral in Parroquia El Bosque, a tall
  old man with a German name came over to Rodrigo, said *"Usted es el doctor de Anselmo,"* and
  then: *"Lo que Anselmo le dio no era de Anselmo."* That was **Gustavo Rehbein**, the only time
  they met. Rodrigo heard later that the old man "tiene lo suyo en La Reina".
- **The Escuela Hora Dorada** (2011): courses for the Network's own crews, then for SAMU,
  Bomberos, mining companies, private clinics. Prehospital tranexamic acid from 2011, years
  before it was routine elsewhere in Chile. The phrase *"protocolo Hora Dorada"* entered ED slang.
- **Unidad Maipú** (2012). **Unidad Independencia** (2013), in La Chimba, near the old hospital
  tunnels, with a small shelter for twenty-five.
- **The registry** (2012): the first trauma registry of any private network in Chile, shared with
  MINSAL.
- **Carolina** made partner at her firm in 2010, the partnership she'd turned down in 2004. She
  designed every unit. She knew what was under each one.

### 2014–2016: the model spreads, the old men go
- **2014. Benavente died**, aged eighty-eight. His study, his plans and, Rodrigo assumed, the coded
  ledger went somewhere in his family.
- **Unidad Puente Alto** (2014). **Unidad Los Leones** in Providencia (2015). Its "nivel
  técnico" below three parking levels was **the best shelter Rodrigo ever built**: sixty people, a
  well, a year of freeze-dried stores, Swiss filters. Only he, Carolina, Gonzalo and the builder
  knew what it was.
- **2015. Hernán Bastías died** of myeloma, aged seventy-four, in the Villa Olímpica flat. In his
  last month he told his son what he had never told anyone. In **1987** a general of engineers,
  Benavente, had asked him to consult on radiation shielding for "unos refugios de gente con
  plata". He'd said no, out of fear, and he had been afraid of those men for the rest of his
  life. *"Y después te fuiste a meter con ellos, mijo."*
- **The model spreads.** The state's own high-resolution urgent-care centres (the SAR, 2015) were
  argued for, in part, with the Network's numbers. Clinic groups copied the stabilize-and-
  transfer unit. The Network had become a school of thought: emergency medicine that goes to the
  patient, decides fast, does what matters now, and hands over well.
- **Unidad Quilicura** (2016). Fleet: thirty-four ambulances, six of them physician-staffed, eight
  motorcycles. About 1,000 staff.
- **July 2016. Seba died.** He was found in the on-call room at Toesca during the winter campaign,
  forty-six years old: benzodiazepines and alcohol. Everyone had known and nobody had said it out
  loud. Rodrigo found him. **The list was never updated again.**

## Part III — El Olvido (2016–2019)

- **The world seemed to calm.** The economy grew, the Copa América was won twice, and the Cold
  War was something in documentaries. Rodrigo's children grew up. He stopped testing the
  shelters' generators every month, then every quarter. His **Preparation** drive, once 15, sank
  to 8.
- **2016:** the Network's **prehospital whole blood** programme, the first in Latin America,
  fought the ISP and the Colegio Médico for two years, and was approved in 2018.
- **Gonzalo** became **CEO** in 2016. Rodrigo stayed chairman and taught.
- **Doña Pilar Ezquerra**, the widow who had once sold La Mesa's gossip, died in 2016. By then the
  only one of the twelve still living was Rehbein.
- **The Huelén lease** kept paying its 45 UF a month for "bodegas" at −3 that nobody visited
  after 2012. Larrañaga's grandson ran the family company from El Golf and signed the cheques.
  Huelén's loan was refinanced and repaid early, in 2015.
- **August 2019. Pincheira died**, aged seventy-five, in a flat in Barrio Yungay. In 2017 Rodrigo
  had hired his grandson **Bastián** as a maintenance technician at Toesca, as a favour to the old
  man.

## Part IV — Estallido, peste, venta (2019–2023)

- **18 October 2019.** The **Plaza Italia unit** stood at the corner of the estallido. For five
  months it treated tear gas, pellets, fractures, burns and **eyes**, sending the worst to the
  Salvador's ocular trauma unit. Volunteer brigades slept in its corridors.
  - Carabineros gassed its entrance twice. Protesters set fire to its gate once.
  - During the curfew nights, when the street was a battlefield, the staff and the wounded went
    down to **B1**. It had been built for the bomb, and in 2019 it served a different purpose.
  - The Network's Management held (the year was a success, barely). Its reputation split: heroes
    to some, *"la clínica de los encapuchados"* to others.
- **COVID-19, 2020–2021.**
  - The Network ran a large share of Santiago's critical transfers for the UGCC at the peak, and
    its ICUs were full for fourteen months.
  - Two TENS from Puente Alto died of COVID.
  - Toesca's **−3** became a dormitory for staff who couldn't go home to their families. For a
    year it held mattresses and the smell of disinfectant, and after that nobody went back down.
- **November 2021. The sale.** COVID losses, Isapre bills unpaid for 200 days, and debt: Hora
  Dorada sold **60%** to **Andes Health Partners**, a US–Colombian healthcare fund.
  - Rodrigo kept 26%, Gonzalo 9%, and Seba's mother 5%.
  - The fund's people read the lease of −3 as a small rent from a family investment company.
- **2022.** Russia invaded Ukraine, and in March the word *nuclear* came back to the news.
  Rodrigo went down to Los Leones' technical level alone one night and found the filters mouldy
  and the water tank green. He wrote a page in his notebook. He didn't fix it.
- **March 2023.** The fund appointed a professional CEO, **Felipe Cáceres**; Gonzalo moved to the
  board.
- **June 2023.** An atmospheric river flooded **Independencia's** shelter to the ceiling. It was
  pumped out and closed.
- **The founders leave.** The people who invented this way of doing emergency medicine began to
  go:
  - **Dra. Macarena Sáez**, Seba's successor as medical director, to Melbourne;
  - **Dr. Nacho Pavez**, who built the motorcycles and the whole blood, to Madrid (2024);
  - **Rodrigo and Carolina**, in **August 2023**, to **Seattle**. There Antonia was finishing a
    PhD in geology. Rodrigo took a visiting professorship in prehospital and disaster medicine at
    a university hospital whose ambulances he'd admired since 1998.
- **The Ñuñoa house** stayed in the family; **Vicente**, 21, a civil engineering student, lives
  there with a friend. The cellar is still there. In it are a locked army-surplus cabinet,
  **fourteen notebooks** (2005–2023), three CDs, the 1985 miner's lamp, and Seba's typed list.
- Rodrigo's last notebook entry before the flight, 14 August 2023:
  *"Alguien tiene que saber. No sé quién."*
